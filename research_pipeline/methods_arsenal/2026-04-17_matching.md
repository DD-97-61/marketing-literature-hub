### Match #1: Dynamic Brand Equity Measurement via LLM-Coded Consumer Reviews

- **Score: 14/15** (Gap: 5 + Method: 4 + Data: 5)
- **Research gap:** Consumer-based brand equity (CBBE) is measured almost exclusively via cross-sectional surveys. There is an explicit call in JM and Marketing Science for panel-based, continuous brand equity tracking from observational data. As LLMs make large-scale text annotation feasible, the gap between measurement aspiration and execution has closed — yet no published paper has operationalized this workflow at scale for brand equity dimensions.
- **Source of gap:** Longstanding measurement gap in brand equity literature (Keller CBBE framework); accelerated by LLM annotation capabilities emerging 2023–2025.
- **Proposed method:** LLM-as-annotator (GPT-4o or Claude) codes each consumer review on four CBBE dimensions (perceived quality, brand associations, brand awareness signals, brand loyalty signals) → aggregate to brand-month panel → panel fixed-effects regression.
- **Data source:** Amazon Customer Reviews Dataset (UCSD McAuley Lab) — 233M+ reviews across 29 product categories, 1996–2023, freely downloadable. Covers thousands of named brands.
- **Feasibility assessment:** High. One researcher can download the Amazon dataset (public), run LLM annotation on a stratified sample (~500K reviews), and build a brand-month panel in 3–4 months. LLM annotation cost at GPT-4o rates is ~$500–800 for this scale. No special data access needed.
- **Target journal:** *Journal of Marketing* or *Marketing Science*
- **Rough research design:**
  - **DV:** Brand equity index (composite of LLM-coded quality, associations, loyalty signals per brand per quarter)
  - **IV:** Brand-level events (new product launches, recalls, marketing campaigns from press releases), competitive dynamics (competitor equity scores)
  - **Method:** LLM annotation → brand-quarter panel → two-way FE regression with robustness checks (alternative LLM prompts, validation against BrandZ scores)
  - **Data:** Amazon reviews (sampled 500K reviews, ~3,000 brands, 2015–2023)
- **Risks/challenges:**
  - LLM annotation validity must be demonstrated (need survey benchmark subset)
  - Amazon = single channel; may not generalize to service brands
  - Prompt sensitivity requires systematic robustness testing
  - Amazon removed bulk download in 2024; verify current access via UCSD McAuley page

### Match #2: How AI-Generated Brand Content Affects Consumer Engagement — A Natural Experiment

- **Score: 12/15** (Gap: 5 + Method: 4 + Data: 3) — **HIGH PRIORITY**
- **Research gap:** Consumers cannot always distinguish AI-generated from human-created brand content; the effect of AIGC adoption on consumer engagement and trust is an explicit and fast-emerging gap in JCR and JMR post-ChatGPT. Papers call for field (not lab) evidence using real platform data.
- **Source of gap:** Multiple JCR/JMR editorials and empirical papers (2024–2025) on AI and consumer behavior signal this as a high-priority open question. The staggered rollout of AIGC tools creates a rare natural experiment window before brands uniformly adopt.
- **Proposed method:** Difference-in-differences (staggered adoption design) exploiting variation in when brands publicly announced adoption of AI content tools + LLM-based content classification to identify AI-generated posts.
- **Data source:** Brand social media posts scraped from Weibo (~50K posts per major brand, 2022–2025). AIGC tool launch/adoption dates from press releases and industry reports (publicly available). Engagement metrics from Weibo public API.
- **Feasibility assessment:** Moderate. The hardest step is building the brand-level AIGC adoption timeline (~2–3 weeks of manual research for 100 brands). Scraping Weibo is technically feasible with available tools. LLM classification of AI-generated content is an active NLP task with decent performance. Total timeline: 5–6 months for a solo researcher.
- **Target journal:** *Journal of Consumer Research* or *Journal of Marketing Research*
- **Rough research design:**
  - **DV:** Per-post engagement rate (likes+comments+shares / followers), sentiment of comments, brand trust signals in comment text
  - **IV:** Treatment = brand adopted AIGC tools (staggered timing), post-level = LLM-classified as AI-generated (binary)
  - **Method:** Staggered DID (Callaway-Sant'Anna estimator to handle heterogeneous treatment timing) + robustness via synthetic control
  - **Data:** Weibo scraped posts for top 150 Chinese consumer brands, 2022–2025 (~7.5M posts total)
- **Risks/challenges:**
  - Identifying true AIGC adoption timing requires manual verification
  - LLM content detector accuracy is imperfect (~85–90%) — attenuation bias concern
  - Confounds: brands that adopt AIGC may also increase posting frequency or shift strategy simultaneously
  - Weibo scraping is technically feasible but ToS grey area; use academic partnerships where possible

### Match #3: Chinese Brand Communication Adaptation in International Markets — Cross-Lingual NLP

- **Score: 12/15** (Gap: 4 + Method: 4 + Data: 4) — **HIGH PRIORITY**
- **Research gap:** The literature on brand internationalization lacks computational, large-scale evidence on how Chinese brands adapt brand communication for foreign markets vs. maintaining global consistency. JIBS and JIM explicitly call for research on Chinese brand internationalization with novel methods and larger samples than case studies allow.
- **Source of gap:** Routine #3 research domain (Brand Internationalization); consistent call in JIBS special issues on emerging market MNCs; brand localization vs. standardization debate remains unresolved with observational data.
- **Proposed method:** Cross-lingual sentence embeddings (Google LaBSE or Meta LASER) to compute semantic similarity between a brand's Chinese-language posts (Weibo) and English-language posts (Instagram/Twitter) for the same brand in overlapping time periods. Cluster brands by adaptation strategy and regress engagement outcomes on adaptation degree.
- **Data source:** (a) Weibo API for Chinese-market brand posts; (b) Instagram Graph API or scraping for international posts. Target: top 100 Chinese brands with active international social media presence (Xiaomi, Haier, Hisense, Li-Ning, Huawei, ByteDance brands, etc.).
- **Feasibility assessment:** Moderate-high. LaBSE is open-source (HuggingFace, no cost). Weibo API requires academic application (obtainable). Instagram scraping is feasible with Apify or similar tools. Cross-lingual embedding comparison is established methodology. Timeline: 4–5 months.
- **Target journal:** *Journal of International Business Studies* or *Journal of International Marketing*
- **Rough research design:**
  - **DV:** Consumer engagement rate (platform-normalized), brand sentiment in comments, cross-cultural brand perception divergence
  - **IV:** Content adaptation score = 1 − cosine_similarity(Weibo post embedding, Instagram post embedding) for matched time windows; brand category, market entry mode, brand age
  - **Method:** LaBSE embeddings → adaptation score time series → fixed-effects panel regression; cluster analysis for brand adaptation strategy types
  - **Data:** ~500K posts across Weibo (Chinese) + Instagram (international) for 100 Chinese brands, 2021–2025
- **Risks/challenges:**
  - Brands may post different content types by platform by design (not just adaptation — could be audience targeting)
  - Instagram API is restricted post-2023; scraping alternatives carry ToS risk
  - Language-agnostic embeddings still have cross-lingual alignment gaps for Chinese
  - Need to control for overall brand quality and international marketing budget (WRDS financial data)

### Match #4: Brand Resilience Early Warning via Social Listening and Anomaly Detection

- **Score: 11/15** (Gap: 3 + Method: 4 + Data: 4)
- **Research gap:** Brand crisis research is overwhelmingly reactive (examining outcomes after a crisis peaks). There is no established framework for detecting early-warning signals from social media time series before a brand crisis escalates. JAMS and Journal of Brand Management papers call for predictive, real-time brand health monitoring.
- **Source of gap:** Routine #4 (Brand Competitiveness) and Routine #5 (Social Media) research domains; brand resilience literature review gap.
- **Proposed method:** Change point detection (Ruptures/PELT algorithm) on Weibo brand mention time series to identify anomalous structural breaks in sentiment velocity, topic coherence, and mention volume. Train a classification model to distinguish pre-crisis signals from noise.
- **Data source:** Weibo brand mention time series (Weibo API) for ~200 major Chinese consumer brands, 2018–2025. Crisis event labels constructed from Wikipedia Chinese brand crisis entries + Baidu news archives (publicly searchable).
- **Feasibility assessment:** Moderate. Weibo API gives historical data at reasonable rate limits. Ruptures is an open-source Python library. Crisis labeling requires ~2–3 weeks manual effort. Training a crisis prediction model with ~100 labeled events is challenging (small N) — consider few-shot LLM classification as alternative. Timeline: 5–6 months.
- **Target journal:** *Journal of the Academy of Marketing Science* or *Journal of Marketing*
- **Rough research design:**
  - **DV:** Whether a structural break in sentiment 7/14/30 days before a crisis peak is detectable (classification task); crisis severity (peak negative sentiment score)
  - **IV:** Sentiment velocity (rate of change), topic drift from baseline, mention volume anomaly, cross-account coordination signals
  - **Method:** Ruptures PELT for change point detection → feature extraction → logistic regression / random forest crisis classifier → precision/recall evaluation
  - **Data:** Weibo time series for 200 brands (daily sentiment aggregates, 2018–2025); ~100 labeled crisis events
- **Risks/challenges:**
  - Crisis event labeling is labor-intensive and subjective (what counts as a "crisis"?)
  - Small number of labeled crisis events limits predictive model generalizability
  - Weibo data completeness: historical data via API may be incomplete pre-2020
  - Weibo-specific findings may not generalize to non-Chinese markets

### Match #5: Influencer–Brand Fit Detection at Scale via Multimodal Embeddings

- **Score: 10/15** (Gap: 4 + Method: 2 + Data: 4)
- **Research gap:** Influencer-brand congruence/fit is consistently identified as a key moderator of influencer marketing effectiveness (JMR, JAMS), but measurement relies on survey-based fit perception. No paper has proposed a computational, content-based fit measure applicable to large samples from real platform data.
- **Source of gap:** Routine #5 (Social Media × Consumer Behavior); influencer marketing literature gap.
- **Proposed method:** CLIP-based multimodal embeddings (text + image) for influencer posts and brand identity posts → cosine similarity as "content fit score" → panel regression of fit score on engagement performance.
- **Data source:** Xiaohongshu (RED) public posts — influencer content + sponsored brand content is publicly visible. Xiaohongshu has ~300M monthly users; brand-sponsored posts are labeled with disclosure tags (Chinese regulations require this since 2022).
- **Feasibility assessment:** Moderate. CLIP is open-source (OpenAI). Xiaohongshu scraping is technically feasible (Python scripts available on GitHub) but ToS is a concern. The main challenge is CLIP's limited training on Chinese text — may need Chinese CLIP (CN-CLIP from BAAI) instead. Timeline: 5–7 months.
- **Target journal:** *Journal of Marketing Research* or *JAMS*
- **Rough research design:**
  - **DV:** Post engagement rate (likes+saves+comments / estimated followers), conversion signals from comments (purchase intent language)
  - **IV:** Content fit score (CN-CLIP cosine similarity, brand vs. influencer content), influencer tier (nano/micro/macro/mega), disclosure salience, product category
  - **Method:** CN-CLIP embeddings → fit score → panel FE regression; robustness via alternative embedding models
  - **Data:** ~200K Xiaohongshu sponsored posts from ~5,000 influencers, 2022–2025
- **Risks/challenges:**
  - CN-CLIP fit score validity for brand-influencer congruence is untested (significant methodological contribution but also a risk)
  - Xiaohongshu ToS restricts scraping; academic partnerships (e.g., CCER datasets) are preferable
  - Follower count not always visible on RED; need proxy for reach normalization
