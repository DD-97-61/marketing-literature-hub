### Match #1: Visual Brand Aesthetic Style Positioning via CLIP Embeddings — Chinese New-Style Tea on Xiaohongshu

- **Score: 14/15** (Gap: 5 + Method: 5 + Data: 4)
- **Research gap:** No published work computationally measures and compares *brand aesthetic styles* (minimalist vs. ornate, Eastern vs. Western, warm vs. cool) across competing brands and links these style dimensions to consumer engagement outcomes. Existing CV work (Liu et al. 2020, Marketing Science) measures brand *perceptual attributes* (glamorous, rugged, healthy) — a fundamentally different construct from aesthetic *style*.
- **Source of gap:** Li, Y., Lee, H.H.M., & Blasco-Arcas, L. (2025, JBR) — "Computer Vision in Branding: A Conceptual Framework and Future Research Agenda" explicitly calls for "progression to integrative visual analysis" beyond attribute detection. Liu et al. (2020, Marketing Science) is the foundational paper whose contribution stops at attribute extraction, leaving style measurement open. Also flagged in `brand_aesthetics_research_assessment.md` Gap 1 as "the most publishable gap."
- **Proposed method:** CLIP-based visual embeddings (OpenAI CLIP, open-source) to extract style representations from brand images → dimensionality reduction (UMAP/PCA) → cluster analysis to discover aesthetic style dimensions → NIMA (Neural Image Assessment, PyTorch, idealo/image-quality-assessment) for absolute aesthetic quality scoring → negative binomial regression linking style dimensions + aesthetic scores to engagement metrics. **PASS criteria:** (1) works with observational data ✓ (2) public data ✓ (3) published examples in IS/CS applied to brand analysis ✓ (4) open-source implementations ✓.
- **Data source:** Xiaohongshu (RED) — scrape image posts + engagement metrics (likes, comments, saves) from 20–30 Chinese new-style tea brand accounts (Heytea, CHAGEE, Nayuki, Lelecha, Bawang Chaji, Guming). Estimated N: 30,000–80,000 posts over 2 years. Instagram as backup/cross-platform validation.
- **Feasibility assessment:** Yes, one researcher in 6 months. Scraping Xiaohongshu via Playwright/Selenium is documented (though requires periodic maintenance). CLIP inference on 80K images takes ~8 hours on a single GPU or ~48 hours on CPU. NIMA is lightweight. Statistical analysis is standard panel regression. Total compute cost: minimal (open-source tools + Google Cloud Vision free tier for object labels).
- **Target journal:** JBR (primary) → Marketing Science (if engagement model is strong) → IJRM
- **Rough research design:**
  - **DV:** Post-level engagement (likes, comments, saves); brand-level average engagement rate
  - **IV:** CLIP aesthetic style dimensions (extracted via PCA/clustering from image embeddings); NIMA aesthetic quality score; visual complexity; color warmth/saturation
  - **Method:** Negative binomial regression (count DVs) with brand fixed effects; UMAP for visualization; cluster analysis for style taxonomy
  - **Data:** Xiaohongshu scraped posts, ~20–30 tea brands, 2-year window, estimated N = 40,000–80,000 images
  - **Controls:** Post recency, posting time, follower count, caption length, hashtag count, product visibility
- **Risks/challenges:**
  - Xiaohongshu scraping is technically feasible but platform ToS may restrict; Xiaohongshu has blocked scrapers periodically. **Mitigation:** Collect data quickly; use rotating IPs; have Instagram as backup.
  - CLIP embeddings capture *semantic* content alongside style — must carefully validate that extracted dimensions reflect aesthetic style not just product category. **Mitigation:** Human validation study on subset of images.
  - Chinese-language captions require Chinese NLP for control variables (sentiment, topic). **Mitigation:** Use jieba + SnowNLP or pre-trained Chinese BERT.
  - Reviewers unfamiliar with Chinese platform context. **Mitigation:** Rich platform description; frame as universal theory (processing fluency + brand positioning) tested in novel context.

### Match #2: Does Visual Consistency Pay? Computational Measurement of Brand Image Coherence on Xiaohongshu

- **Score: 13/15** (Gap: 4 + Method: 5 + Data: 4)
- **Research gap:** Brand visual consistency is a practitioner axiom ("consistent branding builds recognition") but has never been measured computationally on social media, especially not on Chinese platforms. The JBR 2025 CTV-CBBE framework identifies "brand visual harmony across touchpoints" as an unanswered research question. No paper tests whether higher within-brand visual consistency predicts higher engagement at the post or brand level.
- **Source of gap:** Li et al. (2025, JBR) CTV-CBBE framework; also implied by Liu et al. (2020, Marketing Science) variable "brand image gap" (firm vs. UGC) which hints at consistency but does not measure intra-brand consistency over time. `brand_aesthetics_research_assessment.md` Gap 4.
- **Proposed method:** CLIP embeddings for all posts per brand → pairwise cosine similarity within-brand across time window → brand-level visual consistency index → longitudinal panel regression (brand × month) linking consistency to engagement growth. Also: within-post carousel consistency (image 1 vs. image 2…5). **PASS criteria:** all 4 met. Nice-to-have: works on Chinese platform data ✓; no massive compute ✓.
- **Data source:** Xiaohongshu brand accounts — same data collection as Match #1 (synergistic if both studies share data). Can expand to 50+ brands across multiple categories (tea, hospitality, fashion, beauty). Alternatively, Instagram for Western replication sample.
- **Feasibility assessment:** Yes. Computing CLIP embeddings + pairwise similarity within brand is O(N²) within each brand — for 2,000 posts per brand, this is 4M pairs, computationally trivial. Panel regression is standard. Major effort is data collection and cleaning.
- **Target journal:** JMR (primary) → Marketing Science → IJRM
- **Rough research design:**
  - **DV:** Post-level log(likes+1), log(comments+1); brand-level weekly engagement rate
  - **IV:** Brand visual consistency index (mean pairwise CLIP cosine similarity of posts in prior 30 days); visual consistency trend (improving vs. declining)
  - **Moderator:** Brand tier (luxury vs. mass market); brand age on platform
  - **Method:** Two-way fixed effects panel (brand × month); instrumental variable for consistency (post volume as instrument)
  - **Data:** Xiaohongshu, 50+ brands, 24-month window, ~100,000 posts
- **Risks/challenges:**
  - Endogeneity: brands with more resources post more consistently AND get more engagement. **Mitigation:** Brand FE + IV strategy; lagged consistency as IV.
  - Consistency may be confounded with content type diversity (brands posting diverse product types look inconsistent). **Mitigation:** Control for content type mix using object detection labels.
  - Publishing risk: if consistency has NO effect, null results are harder to publish. **Mitigation:** Pre-register or frame as "when does consistency pay?" moderation story.

### Match #3: Eastern Aesthetics as Brand Capital — A Computer Vision Study of Designer Hotels on Social Media

- **Score: 12/15** (Gap: 4 + Method: 4 + Data: 4)
- **Research gap:** "Eastern aesthetics" (东方美学) as a branding strategy is studied qualitatively — case studies, conceptual frameworks, practitioner reports. CV/computational methods are applied almost exclusively to Western brand contexts. No published paper combines CV analysis with the Eastern aesthetics construct in branding. This creates a distinctive contribution: quantifying what "Eastern aesthetic" means visually (color palette, symmetry, natural elements, calligraphic text) and testing whether it drives consumer response differently than "Western modern" aesthetics.
- **Source of gap:** Asian Journal of Communication (2026) — Eastern aesthetics in advertising (conceptual); `brand_aesthetics_research_assessment.md` Gap 2; Li et al. (2025, JBR) call for non-Western brand contexts.
- **Proposed method:** (1) Define "Eastern aesthetic" visual features from art/design literature (muted earth tones, symmetry, natural motifs, negative space, traditional patterns) → (2) Build a CV feature extraction pipeline: color palette extraction (OpenCV HSV), symmetry score, object detection for "natural elements" (bamboo, stone, water, flowers), OCR for Chinese calligraphy text, scene classification (indoor traditional vs. modern) → (3) Create "Eastern aesthetic index" as composite score → (4) Compare hotel brands: Eastern-positioned (HUI Hotel, Ji Xia Shan, SUNYATA) vs. Western-modern-positioned → (5) Regression: Eastern aesthetic index → engagement + review sentiment. **PASS** on secondary data criteria; CONDITIONAL on the "Eastern aesthetic index" construct (requires human validation). Nice-to-have: Chinese language data ✓.
- **Data source:** Xiaohongshu posts from 40–50 designer/boutique hotels in China (half explicitly using Eastern aesthetics, half modern/Western style). Supplement with Ctrip/Meituan hotel image galleries (freely browsable). Estimated N: 20,000–40,000 hotel images + engagement metadata.
- **Feasibility assessment:** Feasible in 6 months with effort. The main challenge is constructing and validating the "Eastern aesthetic index" — requires subject matter knowledge and a human coding validation study (10–20 coders rating 200 images). This is doable. Hotel content on Xiaohongshu is substantial and scraping is established.
- **Target journal:** JBR (primary) → International Journal of Hospitality Management → Tourism Management → JRCS
- **Rough research design:**
  - **DV:** Post engagement (likes, saves, comments on Xiaohongshu); review rating (Ctrip)
  - **IV:** Eastern Aesthetic Index (composite CV score: color warmth/muted palette, symmetry, natural element count, calligraphic text presence, negative space ratio)
  - **Moderator:** Hotel price tier (luxury vs. mid-range); guest demographics (inferred from platform)
  - **Method:** OLS/negative binomial regression; PLS-SEM if survey supplement added
  - **Data:** Xiaohongshu posts from 40–50 hotels; Ctrip hotel image galleries; ~30,000 images
- **Risks/challenges:**
  - "Eastern aesthetic" construct validity — reviewers may challenge operationalization. **Mitigation:** Root in established design literature (harmony, wholeness, naturalness); recruit Chinese design experts for construct validation.
  - Small N at hotel level (40–50 hotels) for brand-level analysis. **Mitigation:** Analyze at image level (N=30K+); brand as cluster-level control.
  - Hospitality journals have different methodological expectations — may need to supplement with qualitative insights or interviews. **Mitigation:** Position for JBR (marketing-focused) rather than pure hospitality journal.

### Match #4: Aesthetic Premium Across Product Categories — NIMA Scoring on Multi-Category Chinese Social Media

- **Score: 11/15** (Gap: 3 + Method: 5 + Data: 3)
- **Research gap:** Philp, Jacobson & Pancer (2022, JBR) showed aesthetic image quality (food typicality via CV) predicts restaurant Instagram engagement. Peng, Eisend & Chen (2025, JM) meta-analysis calls for primary studies testing whether aesthetic effects vary by *product category* and *cultural context* — these are identified moderators with only weak primary study evidence. No multi-category, multi-cultural study uses NIMA (Neural Image Assessment) to measure absolute aesthetic quality across categories on a Chinese platform.
- **Source of gap:** Peng et al. (2025, JM) — explicit call for future research on category and culture moderators; Philp et al. (2022, JBR) calls for extension beyond restaurant food context.
- **Proposed method:** NIMA (Neural Image Assessment — trained on AVA dataset of 255K human-rated images; Python/PyTorch, open-source) to score aesthetic quality of brand posts → cross-category comparison (tea, beauty, fashion, hospitality, food & beverage) → multilevel model with aesthetic score → engagement, with category as moderator. **PASS criteria:** all 4 met. NIMA has published applications in marketing research (Luxury in Focus, JBR 2025).
- **Data source:** Multi-category Xiaohongshu brand posts (harder: requires data across 5+ categories) OR Instagram (single platform, multi-category — easier but less novel). Estimated N: 50,000–100,000 posts across 5 categories (10 brands each). Data availability is 3/5 — requires substantial scraping across categories and platforms.
- **Feasibility assessment:** Feasible in 6–9 months. Main effort is multi-category data collection (more effort than single-category). NIMA is computationally fast (< 2 hours for 100K images on CPU). Multilevel modeling is standard.
- **Target journal:** JBR (primary) → Psychology & Marketing → JAMS
- **Rough research design:**
  - **DV:** Log(likes+1), log(comments+1), engagement rate
  - **IV:** NIMA aesthetic quality score (0–10); image fluency dimensions (simplicity, symmetry, contrast)
  - **Moderator:** Product category (tea, beauty, fashion, hospitality, F&B); brand tier (luxury vs. mass)
  - **Method:** Multilevel model (posts nested in brands nested in categories); random slopes for aesthetic effects across categories
  - **Data:** Xiaohongshu or Instagram, 50 brands across 5 categories, ~80,000 posts
- **Risks/challenges:**
  - NIMA was trained on general aesthetic images (photography community), not brand marketing content. **Mitigation:** Validate NIMA scores against human raters on brand images; report correlation.
  - Cross-category comparison requires sampling equivalence. **Mitigation:** Sample same time period and match brand tier within category.
  - Incremental over Philp et al. (2022, JBR) may be questioned. **Mitigation:** Emphasize NIMA vs. typicality (aesthetic quality vs. category-specific typicality), multi-category design, and Chinese platform novelty.

### Match #6: Multimodal Text-Image Congruence and Engagement — NLP + CV on Brand Posts

- **Score: 8/15** (Gap: 3 + Method: 3 + Data: 2)
- **Gap:** Dang, Kwan, Jia & Shi (2026, JMR) showed text-image congruence in brand posts drives engagement differentially by platform. This is a very recent finding calling for replication in Chinese social media context with different content norms. Gap is implied, not explicitly called out in a UTD24 paper.
- **Method:** CLIP-based text-image similarity (CLIP can embed both text and images in same space → cosine similarity = congruence score) + causal forest for heterogeneous treatment effects. **CONDITIONAL** — CLIP for Chinese text requires Chinese-CLIP (open-source from BAAI); causal forest implementation (grf package in R, EconML in Python) is published. Together these require more integration.
- **Data:** Xiaohongshu posts with both captions and images — same collection challenge. Chinese-CLIP quality on Xiaohongshu content is untested.
- **Why medium not top:** Method is CONDITIONAL (Chinese-CLIP adaptation needed); gap is implied not explicit; data feasibility is uncertain for caption quality.
- **Target journal:** JMR, Marketing Science
- **Next steps needed:** Test Chinese-CLIP on sample of Xiaohongshu data; assess caption quality (many posts have very short captions).
