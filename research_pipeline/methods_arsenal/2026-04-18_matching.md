### Match #1: Visual Brand Aesthetic Style Dimensions — CLIP + NIMA on Chinese Tea Brands

- **Score: 14/15** (Gap: 5 + Method: 5 + Data: 4)
- **Research gap:** No study has computationally measured and compared brand *aesthetic styles* (minimalist vs. ornate, warm vs. cool, cultural heritage vs. modern) — as distinct from brand *attributes* (glamorous, rugged). Liu et al. (2020, Marketing Science) measured perceptual attributes; Li et al. (2025, JBR) explicitly call for progression to "integrative visual analysis" including aesthetic style dimensions.
- **Source of gap:** Liu et al. (2020) Marketing Science; Li et al. (2025) JBR CTV-CBBE framework — both UTD24 outlets.
- **Proposed method:** CLIP image embeddings for style representation → PCA/t-SNE to extract aesthetic dimensions → NIMA (Neural Image Assessment) for overall aesthetic quality scoring → OLS/negative binomial regression linking dimensions to engagement.
- **Data source:** Xiaohongshu brand accounts for 20–30 Chinese new-style tea brands (Heytea, CHAGEE, Nayuki, Lelecha, Cha Baidao, etc.); target ~5,000–15,000 posts with engagement metadata.
- **Feasibility:** High for one researcher. CLIP (open-source, Hugging Face) + NIMA (PyTorch; `idealo/image-quality-assessment` on GitHub) run on CPU/single GPU. Xiaohongshu scraping via Python (Selenium/Playwright); Chinese-language platform adds friction but is manageable.
- **Target journal:** JBR (primary) → IJRM → Marketing Science
- **Rough research design:**
  - **DV:** Post-level engagement (likes, saves, comments); brand-level average aesthetic score
  - **IV:** CLIP-derived aesthetic dimensions (via factor analysis on embeddings); NIMA aesthetic quality score; visual complexity (edge density, color variation)
  - **Moderator:** Brand positioning (new-Chinese/Guochao vs. minimalist-modern vs. Western-inspired)
  - **Method:** Panel OLS with brand fixed effects; robustness via negative binomial for count DVs
  - **Data:** Xiaohongshu scrape; est. N = 10,000+ posts across 25 brands; 2023–2026
- **Risks/challenges:** (1) Xiaohongshu API restrictions — mitigate with Selenium scraping; (2) CLIP dimensions need interpretive labeling via human coding validation; (3) engagement on Xiaohongshu (saves are key metric, not just likes) — must collect saves separately; (4) reviewer unfamiliarity with platform — provide rich institutional context.

### Match #2: HIGH PRIORITY — NIMA Aesthetic Scoring and Brand Engagement Across Product Categories

- **Score: 14/15** (Gap: 4 + Method: 5 + Data: 5)
- **Research gap:** Neural Image Assessment (NIMA), well-validated in computer science (trained on 255K AVA images), has never been applied to brand marketing research. Li & Zhang (2024, JoA) identify "aesthetic quality evaluation" as one of 9 CV image analysis types but flag it as underused in advertising research. Braun et al. / Philp et al. (JBR 2022) used Google Vision confidence scores as a typicality proxy — NIMA provides a direct, model-validated aesthetic quality score that goes further.
- **Source of gap:** Li & Zhang (2024) Journal of Advertising; Philp, Jacobson & Pancer (2022) JBR; assessment document explicit "NOT YET applied to branding in marketing journals."
- **Proposed method:** Apply NIMA (PyTorch, pre-trained on AVA aesthetic dataset) to brand social media images → produce per-image aesthetic quality score (1–10) → regress on engagement controlling for post/brand/time controls.
- **Data source:** Pre-existing Instagram brand post datasets on Kaggle (e.g., "Social Media Influencers" datasets, brand marketing datasets) + Harvard Dataverse (papers with data sharing). Supplemented with fresh Instaloader scraping if needed. Cross-category sampling across food, fashion, luxury, and tea brands.
- **Feasibility:** Very high. NIMA inference runs on CPU in seconds per image. Pre-existing Kaggle datasets eliminate scraping effort for initial analysis. One researcher can run full pipeline in 4–6 weeks.
- **Target journal:** JBR (primary) → Psychology & Marketing → Journal of Advertising
- **Rough research design:**
  - **DV:** Engagement rate (likes + comments / followers); saves (if available)
  - **IV:** NIMA aesthetic quality score (continuous, 1–10)
  - **Moderator:** Product category (food / fashion / luxury / tea); brand tier (mass vs. premium)
  - **Controls:** Post age, follower count, posting time, post type, caption length, hashtag count, face present, brand logo present
  - **Method:** OLS with clustered SEs by brand; moderation via interaction terms; robustness in negative binomial
  - **Data:** Kaggle/Dataverse pre-existing + Instaloader; est. N = 20,000–50,000 posts across 50+ brands
- **Risks/challenges:** (1) NIMA was trained on general photography aesthetics, not brand content — validate with human coders on a brand content subset; (2) Instagram API restrictions mean some datasets may be outdated; (3) potential ceiling effects in highly-curated luxury brand feeds; (4) need to control carefully for brand effects (fixed effects essential).

### Match #3: HIGH PRIORITY — CLIP Visual Positioning Maps: Competitive Aesthetic Differentiation

- **Score: 14/15** (Gap: 5 + Method: 5 + Data: 4)
- **Research gap:** Perceptual/positioning maps have always been attribute-based (surveys, text) or via consumer collages (Dzyabura & Peres 2021, JM). No study has derived a *visual* brand positioning map directly from actual brand content using image embeddings. Liu et al. (2020) mapped brand images to attributes; Li et al. (2025) call for "brand visual consistency" and competitive visual analysis as future work. This is the visual-analytics equivalent of a perceptual map.
- **Source of gap:** Liu et al. (2020) Marketing Science; Li et al. (2025) JBR; Dzyabura & Peres (2021) Journal of Marketing — all top/UTD24 outlets.
- **Proposed method:** CLIP embeddings for all brand posts → average per brand → cosine distance matrix → t-SNE/UMAP 2D projection → visual positioning map. Validate map structure with expert panel (5 brand managers). Link position/distance metrics to brand equity proxies (follower growth, engagement).
- **Data source:** Multi-brand Xiaohongshu + Instagram scrape; tea brands AND hospitality brands for cross-industry richness; ~25–30 brands per industry.
- **Feasibility:** High. CLIP inference is fast (batch processing). Positioning map creation is standard ML pipeline. Main investment is data collection (scraping) and expert validation panel (manageable).
- **Target journal:** JMR or Marketing Science (if strong theoretical framing) → JBR (safer)
- **Rough research design:**
  - **DV:** Visual distance from nearest competitor (continuous); cluster membership (categorical); engagement premium for differentiated positioning
  - **IV:** CLIP-embedding-derived position coordinates; distance to industry centroid
  - **Method:** Cosine distance matrices + UMAP visualization; regression of engagement on visual differentiation; cluster analysis for positioning strategy taxonomy
  - **Data:** 25–30 tea brands + 25–30 hospitality brands on Xiaohongshu; est. N = 30,000+ images
- **Risks/challenges:** (1) CLIP dimensions are not directly interpretable — requires human labeling of map regions; (2) reviewer skepticism about "positioning map" contribution if theory is weak — must anchor in positioning theory (Ries & Trout, Porter competitive differentiation) + processing fluency; (3) cross-industry comparison may dilute focus — consider tea-only first.

### Match #4: HIGH PRIORITY — Brand Visual Consistency (CLIP Pairwise Similarity) and Social Media Engagement

- **Score: 13/15** (Gap: 4 + Method: 5 + Data: 4)
- **Research gap:** Brand visual consistency is a managerial axiom ("keep your brand consistent") but has never been *computationally measured* on social media and linked empirically to consumer engagement outcomes. Li et al. (2025, JBR) explicitly lists brand visual consistency measurement as a key gap. "Luxury in Focus" (JBR 2025) studied fluency within images but not cross-image consistency within a brand's feed.
- **Source of gap:** Li et al. (2025) JBR — explicitly listed as unmeasured; Luxury in Focus (JBR 2025) — by extension.
- **Proposed method:** For each brand: compute pairwise CLIP cosine similarity across all posts → compute brand-level visual consistency score (mean pairwise similarity or variance in embeddings) → link to engagement metrics. Test nonlinearity (too-consistent feeds may become boring).
- **Data source:** Xiaohongshu or Instagram brand feeds; same scrape as Match #1 or #3 — data collection can be shared across studies.
- **Feasibility:** High. Once CLIP embeddings are computed for any brand's posts, consistency scores are trivial to compute. This study can be run in parallel with Match #1 using the same dataset, or as a standalone.
- **Target journal:** IJRM (primary) → JBR → Marketing Science
- **Rough research design:**
  - **DV:** Post-level engagement rate; brand-level average engagement
  - **IV:** Visual consistency score (brand-level: mean pairwise CLIP cosine similarity)
  - **Moderator:** Brand tier (luxury vs. mass); product category; platform
  - **Controls:** Follower count, posting frequency, brand age, post type
  - **Method:** Brand-level cross-sectional OLS; post-level panel with brand FE; test quadratic term for nonlinearity
  - **Data:** Same as Match #1 — 25 brands × ~500 posts = 12,500 posts minimum
- **Risks/challenges:** (1) Causal direction uncertain — brands that get more engagement may learn to be more consistent (reverse causality); mitigate with lagged consistency measures or instrumental variable; (2) "consistency" operationalization needs validation — compare with human-rated consistency on subset; (3) platform algorithm effects may confound — control for recency and posting frequency.

### Match #5: Eastern Aesthetic Element Detection → Brand Attitudes and Engagement (Guochao Brands)

- **Score: 11/15** (Gap: 4 + Method: 3 + Data: 4)
- **Research gap:** Eastern/Chinese aesthetics in branding is studied qualitatively (case studies) and in Western-context CV (object detection, color). No paper applies CV methods to quantitatively measure "Eastern aesthetic" elements in brand visual content and links them to consumer outcomes. Asian Journal of Communication (2026) shows "Traditional Cultural Harmony Imagery" enhances brand attitudes — but relies on experiments. The computational/observational counterpart is missing.
- **Source of gap:** Asian Journal of Communication (2026); Guochao literature; assessment document Gap #2.
- **Proposed method:** CONDITIONAL — two-stage approach: (1) Use GPT-4V / CLIP with text prompts to classify "Eastern aesthetic" elements (calligraphy presence, ink-wash palette, symmetrical traditional motifs, natural elements: bamboo/lotus/mountain, lacquer red) — prompt-based zero-shot classification sidesteps need for fine-tuned model; (2) Regress Eastern aesthetic score on engagement + brand attitudes (proxied via comment sentiment via NLP).
- **Data source:** Xiaohongshu posts from Guochao/新中式 brands (CHAGEE, Li-Ning, Florasis, Perfect Diary) vs. Western-style brands; est. 20 brands × 500 posts = 10,000 images.
- **Feasibility:** Medium. GPT-4V API inference costs ~$0.01/image → $100 for 10K images (manageable). Main challenge is developing and validating the Eastern aesthetic taxonomy and prompt design. Requires Chinese cultural expertise for validation.
- **Target journal:** JBR → Journal of International Marketing → International Marketing Review
- **Rough research design:**
  - **DV:** Engagement rate; comment sentiment (positive/negative via NLP)
  - **IV:** Eastern Aesthetic Score (GPT-4V prompt-based, validated against human coders)
  - **Moderator:** Brand origin (Chinese-native vs. Western brand "going Chinese"); consumer generation (proxy via account followers' demographics if available)
  - **Method:** OLS with brand FE; NLP for comment sentiment DV
  - **Data:** Xiaohongshu; 20 Guochao brands + 10 control brands; 2022–2026
- **Risks/challenges:** (1) CONDITIONAL method — GPT-4V classification requires extensive prompt engineering and validation (est. 200 human-coded images for calibration); (2) defining "Eastern aesthetic" operationally is contested — risk of conceptual vagueness; (3) GPT-4V output consistency needs testing; (4) endogeneity: brands deploying Eastern aesthetics may self-select based on expected consumer response.
