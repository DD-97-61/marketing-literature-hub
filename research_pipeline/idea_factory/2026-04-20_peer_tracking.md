## New Publications by Active Researchers

### Voss, Kevin E.; Mohan, Mayoor; Jung, Jin Ho; Jiménez, Fernando R. — (Multi-institution)
- **Paper:** "The effect of brand alliances across dependent variables and research designs: A meta-analysis"
- **Venue:** *Journal of Business Research*, Vol. 191, 2025
- **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jbusres.2025.000712
- **Topic:** Meta-analysis of 141+ empirical brand alliance studies; finds significant heterogeneity in effect sizes across criterion variables (attitude toward focal brand d=0.20; purchase intention d=0.14; attitude toward alliance d=0.44; perceived quality d=0.26). Key moderator: research design choices (ad format, number of allies) drive effect-size variation more than previously recognized.
- **Overlap with our interests:** **HIGH** — Directly maps our co-branding / brand alliance space. Consolidates three decades of evidence on the exact dependent variables we use (brand attitude, purchase intention, perceived quality). Cites Simonin & Ruth 1998 and Helmig et al. 2008 as foundational anchors.
- **Implication for us:** This is a capstone meta-analysis. Any empirical co-branding paper we submit to JBR or JMR must cite this and explicitly justify how it goes beyond the meta-analytic baseline. **Moderate preemption risk** — if we have a DEVELOP idea focused purely on replicating/extending prior moderators without novel mechanisms or contexts, this paper has already done the synthesis.

## Competitive Landscape Summary

### Getting Crowded
- **Brand alliance meta-analyses:** Two major meta-analyses now anchor the field (Paydas Turan 2021; Voss et al. 2025). Additional meta-analyses in this space face high publication hurdles.
- **Consumer perception of co-branding (survey/SEM):** Fashion co-branding surveys, structural equation models of brand attitude — many teams working here with similar designs.
- **Influencer × brand co-creation:** Rapidly growing stream connecting social media influencers to brand building; becoming crowded with SEM-based studies.

### Still Open
- **Causal identification in brand alliances:** Quasi-experimental or natural experiment designs testing brand alliance effects remain scarce. The meta-analyses highlight that "research design choices" moderate effect sizes — an opening for cleaner causal work.
- **Computational text analysis of co-branding announcements:** NLP-driven analysis of how alliance announcements are received (e.g., social media sentiment, investor reaction) is largely untapped relative to survey approaches.
- **Multi-party brand alliance networks:** Simmons & Durkin (2025) opens this direction but from a qualitative/conceptual lens; quantitative network-analytic approaches are open.
- **Platform-mediated co-branding:** How digital platform architecture (e.g., Amazon, Tmall) shapes co-branding outcomes — minimal formal academic coverage.

### New Entrant Alert
- **Simmons & Durkin (Ulster):** Entering the brand alliance portfolio/network space with *Marketing Letters* 2025 publication — watch for follow-up work on multi-partner alliance effects.
- **Spanish research team (Checa et al.):** Active in fashion co-branding consumer perception; likely to produce further survey-based work in this segment.
