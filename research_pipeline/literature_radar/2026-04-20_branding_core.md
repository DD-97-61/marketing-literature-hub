### The Color of Status: Color Saturation, Brand Heritage, and Perceived Status of Luxury Brands
- **Authors:** Xinyue Zhou, Chunqu Xiao, Sunyee Yoon, Hong Zhu
- **Journal:** Journal of Consumer Research (JCR) | **Year:** 2026 | **Status:** Published (Vol. 52, Issue 6, pp. 1232–1252)
- **Published online:** April 16, 2026
- **DOI / URL:** https://doi.org/10.1093/jcr/ucag010 | https://academic.oup.com/jcr/article/52/6/1232/8120421
- **Abstract summary:** The study investigates how color saturation in product/logo design shapes consumers' perceptions of luxury brand status. Less saturated (i.e., more muted/faded) colors trigger an association with the passage of time, leading consumers to perceive the brand as having a deep continuity heritage. This perceived heritage then elevates the brand's status in consumers' eyes.
- **Methodology:** Seven experimental studies and one field study; controlled experiments manipulating color saturation and brand positioning cues.
- **Key findings:** Low (vs. high) color saturation increases a luxury brand's perceived status via perceived continuity heritage. The effect is attenuated when the brand highlights a recent founding year and is reversed when the brand is positioned as innovative/cutting-edge. Color saturation also predicts willingness to pay and actual product choices.
- **Research gap signaled:** Cross-cultural differences in color-status associations; effects in purely digital/screen-mediated brand contexts; interaction with AI-generated brand imagery; applicability to non-luxury and sub-brand contexts.
- **Relevance:** ★★★★★ — Directly addresses brand heritage and brand identity signaling through design, with strong experimental methodology and clear managerial implications for luxury branding.

### Signaling Sequel Success: How Franchise Characteristics Shape the Influence of Brand Consistency on Movie Sequel Performance
- **Authors:** Brandon Zachary Holle, Ahmet Halil Kirca, Jamal Shamsie, Daniel Chaffin
- **Journal:** Journal of the Academy of Marketing Science (JAMS) | **Year:** 2026 | **Status:** Online-first
- **Published online:** ~April 15, 2026 (confirmed as published ~5 days before search date)
- **DOI / URL:** https://doi.org/10.1007/s11747-026-01148-2 | https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11747-026-01148-2
- **Abstract summary:** Drawing on signaling theory, the paper investigates how brand consistency in movie sequels functions as a quality signal to audiences, and how the strength and reception of that signal is contingent upon franchise-level characteristics. Using a sample of 466 movie sequels, the study tests how franchise length, franchise age, and franchise origin moderate the brand consistency–performance relationship.
- **Methodology:** Secondary data analysis; sample of 466 movie sequels; signaling theory as the theoretical lens; multivariate regression modeling with interaction terms for franchise characteristics.
- **Key findings:** Brand consistency is positively associated with opening weekend box office performance. The impact of this signal is moderated by franchise length, age, and origin—providing evidence that signal quality and strength are context-dependent. Longer or older franchises benefit more from consistency as a signal because audiences have richer brand expectations.
- **Research gap signaled:** Extension to streaming platforms (Netflix, Disney+); international/cross-cultural markets; non-entertainment product/service brand extensions; brand consistency in social media-native brand ecosystems.
- **Relevance:** ★★★★☆ — Brand consistency and brand signaling are central to co-branding and brand extension theory; the franchise context is a novel setting that generates transferable theoretical insights.

### From Branding to Dupes: The New Copyconomy (Editorial)
- **Authors:** Katrijn Gielens (Editor-in-Chief, Journal of Retailing)
- **Journal:** Journal of Retailing (JR) | **Year:** 2026 | **Status:** Published (Vol. 102, pp. 311–314)
- **Published online:** 2026 (exact date within last ~14 days; recently announced on social media)
- **DOI / URL:** https://ideas.repec.org/s/eee/jouret.html (IDEAS/RePec listing); full text via ScienceDirect (abstract only verified)
- **Abstract summary:** The editorial introduces the concept of the "copyconomy"—a fast-growing retail phenomenon in which products deliberately imitating the aesthetic of luxury or iconic brands (commonly called "dupes") have shifted from stigmatized counterfeits to mainstream, socially celebrated purchases. Gielens connects this trend to core branding questions about brand identity, brand equity dilution, and competitive positioning.
- **Methodology:** Conceptual editorial; synthesizes industry data and existing retail/branding literature.
- **Key findings:** Dupe culture challenges traditional brand equity assumptions; brands must reconsider authenticity-based positioning strategies. The "copyconomy" signals structural shifts in how brand associations are formed and leveraged by both originals and imitators.
- **Research gap signaled:** Empirical investigation of brand equity erosion under dupe competition; consumer psychology of dupe choice and guilt/pride; legal, ethical, and strategic responses for incumbent brands; digital platforms' role in amplifying dupe visibility.
- **Relevance:** ★★★★★ — Directly relevant to brand identity, brand authenticity, and competitive branding research; opens an important new empirical agenda.

### The Evolution of Modern Branding: Historical Origins, Current Perspectives, and Future Directions
- **Authors:** Kevin Lane Keller
- **Journal:** Journal of Business Research (JBR) | **Year:** 2025 | **Status:** Published (Vol. 194, 2025, Article 115367)
- **DOI / URL:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jbusres.2025.115359 | https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0148296325001821
- **Abstract summary:** Keller's sweeping review divides 70 years of branding research into three eras: (1) roots of modern branding (1955–1979), (2) branding as a management priority (1980–1999), and (3) personal, social, cultural, and digital dimensions of branding (2000–2025). Five priority research areas are identified for the next generation of branding scholarship.
- **Methodology:** Literature review / perspective piece.
- **Key findings:** Five future priorities: AI and brands; the role/function/scope of branding; brand platforms; brand journeys; brand value and attribution. Digital transformation is repositioning branding from firm-controlled to co-created and algorithmically mediated.
- **Research gap signaled:** AI-generated brand content; brand platform dynamics; multi-stakeholder brand journeys; causal attribution of brand value to financial outcomes.
- **Relevance:** ★★★★★ — Landmark review by a field-defining scholar; directly maps to multiple keyword clusters (digital branding, brand identity, brand equity, brand platform). Essential background reading. (Non-target journal; include for reference.)
