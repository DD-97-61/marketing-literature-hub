### Targeted Advertising in Generative Artificial Intelligence Chatbots: A New Public Health Risk
- **Authors:** [Author names not available in abstract]
- **Venue:** The Lancet | **Year:** 2026
- **AI technology involved:** Generative AI / LLM (ChatGPT-style chatbots)
- **Brand/marketing application:** Branded advertising placement within AI conversational interfaces; implications of chatbot-embedded sponsored content
- **Abstract summary:** As OpenAI rolls out advertising within free and low-cost versions of ChatGPT, this commentary raises public health concerns about targeted advertising embedded in AI chatbots. The authors note that ads appear below AI responses (labeled as sponsored) and are matched to users based on conversation history and past ad interactions, introducing novel risks around influence, transparency, and user autonomy. Voluntary safeguards (privacy protections, under-18 exclusion, separation from responses) are critiqued as insufficient.
- **Methodology:** Policy commentary / conceptual analysis of ad-model mechanics
- **Key findings:** Embedding targeted ads in AI chatbots creates a qualitatively new influence environment where the persuasion boundary between organic AI advice and sponsored content may be blurred, posing risks beyond traditional digital advertising.
- **Potential for our research:** Opens a new empirical agenda — how do consumers perceive brand credibility and advertising disclosure when ads are surfaced inside LLM responses? Could motivate experimental designs comparing chatbot-embedded vs. traditional display advertising.
- **Relevance:** ★★★★★

### Brands Cannot Own a Human Celebrity but Can Own an AI Endorser: AI Endorsers' Role in Shaping Consumer Self-Brand Connection
- **Authors:** [Author names not available in abstract]
- **Venue:** Journal of Retailing and Consumer Services | **Year:** 2025
- **AI technology involved:** AI-generated spokesperson / virtual endorser
- **Brand/marketing application:** AI endorser identity (brand-created vs. public AI persona) and its effect on consumer self-brand connection, brand attitude, and parasocial interaction
- **Abstract summary:** This study distinguishes between brand-created and publicly available AI endorsers, arguing that brands achieve meaningful competitive advantage by owning exclusive AI endorser identities. It tests how endorser type (brand-created vs. public AI) influences consumer self-brand connection through perceived endorser-brand match and parasocial interaction. Results show brand-created AI endorsers generate stronger self-brand connection via a chain-mediation mechanism.
- **Methodology:** Quantitative experiment; SEM with chain mediation analysis
- **Key findings:** Brand-created AI endorsers outperform public AI personas on self-brand connection; perceived endorser-brand match and parasocial interaction serially mediate this effect.
- **Potential for our research:** Directly relevant to strategic AI brand asset design — the "own vs. rent" AI endorser decision. Could be extended with observational data from brand social media campaigns featuring different AI endorser types.
- **Relevance:** ★★★★★

### Evolution, Challenges, and Opportunities of AI-Generated Advertising
- **Authors:** Tae Hyun Baek, Eleonora Pantano, Kirk Plangger, Jungkeun Kim
- **Venue:** International Journal of Advertising, Vol. 45, No. 1 | **Year:** 2026
- **AI technology involved:** LLMs (ChatGPT, Gemini), generative AI, immersive virtual environments
- **Brand/marketing application:** Comprehensive mapping of how AI-generated advertising (AIGA) is reshaping creative production, message effectiveness, and consumer response
- **Abstract summary:** This editorial introduction to a special issue synthesizes the state of AI-generated advertising research and practice. It characterizes AIGA across three capability layers — large language models, computer vision/generative imagery, and immersive environments — and identifies theoretical gaps around consumer processing, ethical governance, and cross-cultural effects. The authors propose an AIGA research agenda with theoretical and managerial priorities.
- **Methodology:** Systematic narrative review / research agenda paper
- **Key findings:** AIGA is now deeply integrated in the advertising ecosystem; core open questions concern consumer cognitive processing of AI-generated vs. human-generated creative, disclosure effects, and governance frameworks.
- **Potential for our research:** Provides a useful taxonomy and agenda-setting framework for positioning our own empirical work. The proposed research questions on disclosure effects and brand authenticity are directly actionable.
- **Relevance:** ★★★★★

### Generative AI and Content Homogenization: The Case of Digital Marketing
- **Authors:** Chaoran Liu, Tong Wang, S. Alex Yang
- **Venue:** SSRN Working Paper (preprint) | **Year:** 2025
- **AI technology involved:** LLMs (generative AI for marketing content creation)
- **Brand/marketing application:** Brand differentiation and uniqueness under mass GenAI content adoption; consumer engagement with AI-homogenized marketing content
- **Abstract summary:** This paper identifies a structural risk from widespread GenAI adoption in marketing: because many brands converge on the same LLMs, AI-generated marketing content may become increasingly similar, dampening consumer engagement and eroding brand distinctiveness. The authors model the homogenization mechanism theoretically and provide empirical evidence from digital marketing contexts showing that content similarity has measurable negative effects on engagement metrics.
- **Methodology:** Theoretical modeling + secondary data analysis (observational digital marketing data)
- **Key findings:** GenAI adoption leads to measurable content homogenization; brand uniqueness advantage from AI content creation diminishes as market-level adoption rises; consumer engagement is negatively affected by perceived content similarity.
- **Potential for our research:** Highly actionable — uses observational secondary data (rare in this space). Directly relevant to our interest in AI + brand differentiation. Motivates research on AI content strategy heterogeneity and first-mover advantages.
- **Relevance:** ★★★★★

### Understanding Customer Responses to AI-Driven Personalized Journeys: Impacts on the Customer Experience
- **Authors:** K. Hardcastle, L. Vorster, D. Brown
- **Venue:** Journal of Advertising, Vol. 54, No. 2 | **Year:** 2025
- **AI technology involved:** AI-driven recommendation and personalization systems
- **Brand/marketing application:** How AI-personalized customer journeys (recommendations, ads) shape customer experience across brand touchpoints
- **Abstract summary:** Using phenomenological semi-structured interviews and Customer Journey Mapping, this study captures how consumers experience AI-driven personalization in brand advertising contexts. It identifies tensions between personalization benefits (relevance, convenience) and consumer empowerment concerns (autonomy, surveillance). Findings reveal how technological limitations of AI mediate brand-customer relationships and influence customer behaviors.
- **Methodology:** Qualitative phenomenology; semi-structured interviews + Customer Journey Mapping
- **Key findings:** Consumers experience ambivalence: they value AI-driven personalization for relevance yet feel disempowered by perceived surveillance; brands must balance personalization depth with transparency to sustain positive experiences.
- **Potential for our research:** Provides rich qualitative grounding for survey/experimental designs on AI personalization and brand trust. The tension between personalization and autonomy is ripe for quantitative modeling.
- **Relevance:** ★★★★☆

### Virtual Influencers: Definition and Future Research Directions
- **Authors:** Audrezet et al. (Special Issue Editors)
- **Venue:** Journal of Business Research, Vol. 200 | **Year:** 2025
- **AI technology involved:** Computer-generated imagery (CGI), generative AI, avatar technology
- **Brand/marketing application:** Defining the virtual influencer construct and mapping its implications for brand endorsement, consumer engagement, and marketing strategy
- **Abstract summary:** This editorial for a major JBR special issue provides the first consolidated definition of virtual influencers and a systematic typology (virtual entertainers, brand-owned avatars, AI-autonomous agents). It synthesizes three research themes from the special issue: human vs. virtual influencer comparisons, virtual influencer characteristics and consumer engagement, and societal/ethical implications. Future research directions include autonomous virtual influencers and cross-media expansion.
- **Methodology:** Systematic literature review / research synthesis
- **Key findings:** Virtual influencers form a distinct endorser category; key moderators are realism level, perceived autonomy, and brand ownership structure; ethical questions around deception and identity representation remain underexplored.
- **Potential for our research:** Serves as a definitive reference for virtual influencer construct definition. The proposed typology (brand-owned vs. public vs. autonomous AI) connects directly to the endorser-ownership question and could inform a meta-analysis across influencer types.
- **Relevance:** ★★★★☆

### LLM-Generated Ads: From Personalization Parity to Persuasion Superiority
- **Authors:** [Authors listed on arXiv]
- **Venue:** arXiv preprint (cs.CL / cs.AI) | **Year:** 2025 (December)
- **AI technology involved:** LLMs (GPT-class models for ad copy generation)
- **Brand/marketing application:** Comparative persuasiveness of LLM-generated vs. human-written advertising copy; testing psychological persuasion principles in AI ad generation
- **Abstract summary:** This preprint examines whether LLMs can generate advertising copy that not only matches but surpasses human-created ads in persuasive effectiveness. Experiments tested LLM ad performance across four foundational psychological persuasion principles — authority, consensus, cognition (need for cognition), and scarcity — finding that AI-generated ads significantly outperformed human-created content at a 59.1% preference rate in direct comparison studies.
- **Methodology:** Online controlled experiment; preference elicitation with human judges; A/B framing across persuasion principle conditions
- **Key findings:** LLM-generated advertising copy achieves persuasion superiority over human-written copy (59.1% preference rate); effect is especially pronounced for scarcity and authority appeals.
- **Potential for our research:** Strong empirical benchmark for AI vs. human ad creative quality. Could be extended by exploring brand fit and brand equity implications of AI-generated persuasive framing.
- **Relevance:** ★★★★☆

### How Do Users Adopt AI-Generated Content (AIGC)? An Exploration of Content Cues and Interactive Cues
- **Authors:** C. X. Li, Y. X. Lin, R. Q. Chen
- **Venue:** Technology in Society, Vol. 81 | **Year:** 2025
- **AI technology involved:** AI-generated content (AIGC) platforms; anthropomorphic AI design
- **Brand/marketing application:** Consumer adoption of brand-produced AIGC; role of perceived intelligence and anthropomorphism in shaping engagement and acceptance
- **Abstract summary:** Grounded in the Information Adoption Model (IAM) augmented with a Cognition-Motivation-Emotion framework, this study examines how content cues (perceived intelligence, anthropomorphism) and interactive cues (performance expectancy, effort expectancy) drive AIGC adoption. Findings show performance expectancy mediates content cues → adoption, with emotion as a key sequential mediator. Anthropomorphism of AI content designs significantly moderates adoption pathways.
- **Methodology:** Structural Equation Modeling (SEM); online survey with consumer panel
- **Key findings:** Perceived intelligence and anthropomorphism in AIGC content shape adoption through performance expectancy and positive emotion; anthropomorphic cues can substitute for or amplify argument quality in driving adoption.
- **Potential for our research:** Useful framework for understanding why consumers accept or reject AI-produced brand content. The anthropomorphism finding links to AI endorser and virtual brand agent research.
- **Relevance:** ★★★★☆

### The Impact of Human and Virtual Endorsements on Advertising Effectiveness: A Comparative Analysis Based on Different Information Appeals
- **Authors:** [Author names from Humanities and Social Sciences Communications]
- **Venue:** Humanities and Social Sciences Communications (Nature Portfolio) | **Year:** 2025
- **AI technology involved:** Virtual/CGI endorser; comparative AI vs. human spokesperson design
- **Brand/marketing application:** Differential effectiveness of human vs. virtual brand endorsers across rational vs. emotional ad appeals
- **Abstract summary:** This study conducts a controlled experiment comparing human versus virtual endorsers across rational-appeal and emotional-appeal advertising conditions, measuring brand trust, attitude, and purchase intention. In rational appeal ads, virtual endorsers enhance brand trust more effectively than human endorsers. In emotional appeal ads, the advantage reverses — human endorsers outperform virtual ones.
- **Methodology:** 2 (endorser type: human vs. virtual) × 2 (appeal type: rational vs. emotional) between-subjects experiment
- **Key findings:** Endorser type × appeal type interaction is significant: virtual endorsers excel on trust in rational ads; human endorsers dominate in emotional appeal contexts. Brands should match AI/human endorser selection to creative appeal strategy.
- **Potential for our research:** Directly actionable for creative strategy guidance on AI endorsers. The appeal-type moderator suggests a contingency model worth developing further with field data.
- **Relevance:** ★★★★☆

- **Abstract summary:** This bibliometric analysis maps four decades of AI-branding research, identifying a peak in publication output in 2023.
