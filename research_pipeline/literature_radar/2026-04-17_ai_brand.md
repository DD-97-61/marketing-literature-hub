### Sustained Impact of Agentic Personalisation in Marketing: A Longitudinal Case Study
- **Authors:** Olivier Jeunen, Eleanor Hanna, Schaun Wheeler
- **Venue:** arXiv preprint (cs.LG / cs.AI) | **Year:** April 2026
- **arXiv ID:** 2604.08621
- **AI technology involved:** Agentic AI; autonomous multi-agent orchestration; reinforcement-learning-based personalisation
- **Brand/marketing application:** CRM messaging optimisation — sustaining engagement lift when human oversight is reduced or removed
- **Abstract summary:** Customer Relationship Management has long relied on manually curated, rule-based messaging. This paper presents an 11-month longitudinal case study of a real-world consumer application in which agentic infrastructure autonomously personalises marketing messages for a large-scale user base. The study compares an "active" phase (human marketers directly curating content and strategy) with a "passive" phase (agents operating from a fixed component library with no human intervention).
- **Methodology:** Quasi-experimental longitudinal design; A/B measurement of engagement metrics (notification click-through, days-with-engagement per user, purchase intent lift) across two distinct operational phases spanning 11 months.
- **Key findings:** Active human management produced the highest relative lift; however, autonomous agents successfully sustained a +57% (±0.11) lift in notification clicks and a +2.4% (±0.21) lift in days-with-engagement during the passive period — demonstrating that agentic systems can maintain meaningful performance without continuous human oversight.
- **Potential for our research:** This is a rare piece of large-scale observational evidence for agentic AI in marketing. The passive-phase results have direct implications for how brands might delegate brand-voice consistency and audience targeting to autonomous agents, opening questions about brand equity maintenance under reduced human control.
- **Relevance:** ★★★★★

### Agentic AI in Branding: Driving Hyper-personalization and Real-time Adaptation
- **Authors:** Sandeep Puri, Jaideep A. Pradhan, Bernadette G. Bernabe, Arnab K. Ray
- **Venue:** Global Business Review (SAGE) | **Year:** January 2026
- **DOI:** 10.1177/09721509251414773
- **AI technology involved:** Agentic AI; autonomous decision-making systems; adaptive learning
- **Brand/marketing application:** Brand identity adaptation; hyper-personalised consumer engagement; brand co-creation
- **Abstract summary:** The paper argues that agentic AI — autonomous systems capable of independent decision-making, learning, and adaptation — is redefining branding by transforming passive consumer interactions into dynamic, co-creative relationships. Drawing on a synthesis of 2020–2024 research and case studies from Coca-Cola, Netflix, and Unilever, it explores how autonomous AI enables hyper-personalised brand experiences while surfacing ethical challenges in consumer engagement.
- **Methodology:** Conceptual/integrative review with illustrative case studies; governance framework development.
- **Key findings:** Agentic AI enhances customer loyalty and innovation when deployed with transparent governance (defined policies, audit checkpoints, escalation protocols); risks of algorithmic bias and brand inconsistency are manageable but require formal oversight mechanisms.
- **Potential for our research:** Provides a practitioner-grounded conceptual map of agentic AI's role in branding. The governance framework is a useful scaffold for empirical studies on brand consistency maintenance in agentic deployments.
- **Relevance:** ★★★★☆

### Evolution, Challenges, and Opportunities of AI-Generated Advertising
- **Authors:** Baek, T. H., Pantano, E., Plangger, K., Kim, J. (editorial team + contributing authors)
- **Venue:** International Journal of Advertising, Vol. 45, No. 1 | **Year:** 2026
- **DOI:** 10.1080/02650487.2025.2612445
- **AI technology involved:** Generative AI; large language models; image-synthesis models
- **Brand/marketing application:** AI-generated ad creative; brand narrative; consumer response to AI-produced advertising content
- **Abstract summary:** This special-issue introductory article maps the current landscape of AI-generated advertising, cataloguing evolutions from early computational creative tools to modern generative models. It identifies key challenges — misinformation, algorithmic bias, legal ambiguity, privacy — while framing opportunities for AI as a "quasi-stakeholder" in value co-creation. The article situates six research needs that frame the full special issue.
- **Methodology:** Systematic literature synthesis and editorial framework-building.
- **Key findings:** AI-generated advertising is transitioning from novelty to norm; over-reliance risks brand voice homogenisation; ethical guardrails and regulatory clarity are the two most pressing research gaps.
- **Potential for our research:** A high-visibility roadmap paper that legitimises the field and provides citation anchors for any study on GenAI advertising effects. Especially relevant if our work addresses brand authenticity or content regulation.
- **Relevance:** ★★★★☆

### Consumer Empowerment Through Generative Artificial Intelligence: Enhancing Brand Narrative with Collaboration, Creation, and Communication
- **Authors:** Yang Feng, Yuan Sun
- **Venue:** International Journal of Advertising | **Year:** 2025 (online first; in IJA Vol. 45, 2026 issue)
- **DOI:** 10.1080/02650487.2025.2547545
- **AI technology involved:** Generative AI (ChatGPT-style tools); consumer co-creation platforms
- **Brand/marketing application:** Brand narrative co-creation; consumer empowerment; purchase intention
- **Abstract summary:** Very little is known about how inviting consumers to co-create brand content with GenAI affects consumer empowerment and advertising outcomes. The authors identify three key constructs in GenAI-assisted co-creation — collaboration, creation, and communication — through 17 in-depth interviews with experienced GenAI users, then validate these and their downstream effects in a survey of 326 participants.
- **Methodology:** Mixed-method: qualitative in-depth interviews (n = 17) for construct discovery; quantitative survey (n = 326) for validation and path modelling.
- **Key findings:** Collaboration and communication with GenAI enhance consumer empowerment (which then improves brand attitudes and purchase intention); "creation" shows an inverted-U relationship with empowerment, suggesting moderate creative involvement is optimal.
- **Potential for our research:** Directly relevant to brand co-creation strategy in the GenAI era. The inverted-U finding on creation is a nuanced result that warrants replication and boundary-condition testing across product categories or brand types.
- **Relevance:** ★★★★★

### Integrating Artificial Intelligence with Market Research: A Dual Approach to Boosting Brand Value
- **Authors:** M. Skare et al.
- **Venue:** Journal of Innovation & Knowledge | **Year:** 2026
- **DOI:** 10.1016/j.jik.2025.100xxx (available via ScienceDirect pii S2444569X25001982)
- **AI technology involved:** AI stock / AI capabilities index; macro-level AI diffusion measurement
- **Brand/marketing application:** Brand equity; brand value creation at the firm and country level
- **Abstract summary:** Using a panel dataset spanning 26 years across 30 countries, this study applies Arellano-Bond and Blundell-Bond System GMM estimations to examine the relationship between national AI stock, AI impact, and aggregate brand value. It proposes a dual-path model in which AI both directly strengthens brand value and indirectly does so through gross value-added (GVA) productivity gains.
- **Methodology:** Macro-panel econometric analysis; System GMM to address endogeneity; 26-year, 30-country longitudinal dataset.
- **Key findings:** AI stock and AI impact each significantly contribute to brand value; their interaction produces synergistic effects beyond individual contributions; AI-driven productivity gains (GVA) partially mediate the AI–brand-value relationship.
- **Potential for our research:** Rare secondary/observational macro-panel approach — a contrast to lab experiments. Could inspire country- or industry-level analyses pairing AI adoption indicators with brand equity data (e.g., Interbrand rankings). The GMM framework is directly adaptable.
- **Relevance:** ★★★★☆

### Brands Cannot Own a Human Celebrity but Can Own an AI Endorser: AI Endorsers' Role in Shaping Consumer Self-Brand Connection
- **Authors:** [Author names not confirmed in search; JRCS article pii S0969698925004965]
- **Venue:** Journal of Retailing and Consumer Services | **Year:** 2025
- **DOI:** available via ScienceDirect pii S0969698925004965
- **AI technology involved:** AI-generated virtual endorsers; brand-controlled AI personas
- **Brand/marketing application:** Brand endorsement; consumer self-brand connection; brand ownership of AI talent
- **Abstract summary:** This paper distinguishes brand-created AI endorsers (owned by a single brand, e.g., a brand's proprietary virtual spokesperson) from public AI endorsers (shared across brands), arguing that exclusive brand ownership of an AI endorser creates a differentiated advantage. The study explores how each type shapes consumer self-brand connection through endorser–brand match and parasocial interaction.
- **Methodology:** Experimental design testing the effect of brand-created vs. public AI endorsers on self-brand connection, mediated by endorser–brand match and parasocial interaction.
- **Key findings:** Brand-created AI endorsers generate stronger consumer self-brand connection than public AI endorsers; the chain-mediation path through endorser–brand match → parasocial interaction drives this advantage.
- **Potential for our research:** Highly actionable for brand strategy research. Raises new empirical questions about exclusivity premiums for branded AI endorsers and their durability over time as AI-generated characters proliferate.
- **Relevance:** ★★★★★

### Virtual Influencers: Definition and Future Research Directions
- **Authors:** Alice Audrezet, Bernadett Koles, Julie Guidry Moulard, Nisreen Ameen, Brad McKenna
- **Venue:** Journal of Business Research, Vol. 200 | **Year:** November 2025
- **DOI:** 10.1016/j.jbusres.2025.115647 (ScienceDirect pii S0148296325004709)
- **AI technology involved:** Computer-generated imagery (CGI); AI-driven persona creation; virtual avatar technology
- **Brand/marketing application:** Brand endorsement via virtual influencers; consumer–brand relationship; brand authenticity perception
- **Abstract summary:** This special-issue anchor article provides a rigorous conceptual definition of virtual influencers as "AI/CGI-generated digital personas adopted by brands for scalable, adaptable, and risk-controlled influence." It maps the existing literature, synthesises findings on consumer perception (authenticity, anthropomorphism, parasocial connection), and identifies a research agenda for the field.
- **Methodology:** Conceptual literature synthesis; introduction to JBR special issue with structured research agenda.
- **Key findings:** Consumers perceive brands endorsed by virtual influencers as less authentic than those endorsed by human influencers; anthropomorphisation and digital escapism co-exist and both matter; blame attribution differs by influencer type in brand-failure scenarios.
- **Potential for our research:** The definitive definitional reference for virtual-influencer research. The future-directions section is a blueprint for empirical designs. Of particular interest: the call for research beyond social media contexts.
- **Relevance:** ★★★★☆

### Authenticity, Ethics, and Transparency in Virtual Influencer Marketing: A Cross-Cultural Analysis of Consumer Trust and Engagement
- **Authors:** [Author names not confirmed; Acta Psychologica, pii S0001691825008868]
- **Venue:** Acta Psychologica | **Year:** 2025
- **DOI:** available via ScienceDirect pii S0001691825008868
- **AI technology involved:** Virtual influencers (AI/CGI personas); disclosure and transparency mechanisms
- **Brand/marketing application:** Consumer trust formation; brand engagement in multicultural contexts; influencer marketing ethics
- **Abstract summary:** This systematic literature review synthesises cross-cultural research on authenticity, ethics, and transparency in virtual influencer marketing. It introduces the Virtual Influencer Trust and Engagement Model (VITEM), which maps relationships between perceived authenticity, disclosure transparency, and cultural context, positioning trust as the critical mediating mechanism. The review highlights significant cross-cultural variation in consumer responses.
- **Methodology:** Systematic literature review with meta-framework construction (VITEM).
- **Key findings:** Disclosure transparency amplifies the positive effect of authenticity on trust; the uncanny valley effect is culturally moderated; consistent digital authenticity, balanced disclosure, appropriate human-likeness, and cultural tailoring are the four main determinants of virtual influencer effectiveness.
- **Potential for our research:** The VITEM framework is directly adaptable as a hypothesis scaffold. Cross-cultural moderation is an underexplored gap — particularly valuable if our research spans Asian and Western markets.
- **Relevance:** ★★★★☆
