### Brands Cannot Own a Human Celebrity but Can Own an AI Endorser: AI Endorsers' Role in Shaping Consumer Self-Brand Connection
- **Authors:** [Not publicly disclosed in accessible sources; authors affiliated with institutions indexed on ScienceDirect]
- **Venue:** Journal of Retailing and Consumer Services | **Year:** 2025/2026 (online first)
- **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jretconser.2025.004965
- **AI technology involved:** AI-generated brand endorsers (CGI + generative AI personas)
- **Brand/marketing application:** How brand-created vs. public AI endorsers differentially shape consumer self-brand connection
- **Abstract summary:** Distinguishes between brand-created AI endorsers (owned exclusively by one brand) and public AI endorsers (shared across brands). Five studies demonstrate that brand-created AI endorsers generate a stronger self-brand connection than public counterparts, mediated through a chain of perceived endorser-brand match → parasocial interaction. The effect is attenuated when consumers have a high need to belong or when the brand adopts a friend-like relational role.
- **Methodology:** Five experimental studies; chain mediation analysis; moderation by need to belong and brand relationship norms
- **Key findings:** Brand-owned AI endorsers outperform public AI endorsers in driving self-brand connection; brand managers who develop proprietary AI endorsers gain a relational advantage not available with licensed celebrity AI personas.
- **Potential for our research:** Directly relevant to brand equity strategies in the AI age — provides a theoretical rationale for brands to invest in proprietary AI personas rather than licensing existing ones. Could inspire research on how "AI brand ownership" signals authenticity vs. commercialism.
- **Relevance:** ★★★★★

### The Power of AI-Generated Content: Evidence From the Peer-to-Peer Accommodation Market
- **Authors:** Ningyuan Fan, Xiang (Robert) Li, Chao Liu, Zhi-Ping Fan
- **Venue:** Journal of Travel Research (SAGE) | **Year:** 2026
- **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1177/00472875251332951
- **AI technology involved:** Large language models (LLMs) as language assistant and content creator
- **Brand/marketing application:** AIGC's impact on listing performance, booking volume, and revenue — field evidence on whether LLM-generated marketing copy works
- **Abstract summary:** Employs a prediction–interpretation–inference machine learning framework on Airbnb listing data to evaluate how AI-generated listing descriptions affect host performance under two conditions: LLM as a language assistant vs. LLM as a full content creator. Finds that prompt design strategy critically determines AIGC effectiveness. AIGC lifted revenue and bookings for underperforming listings but showed limited additional gains for already high-performing listings.
- **Methodology:** Machine learning framework (prediction + SHAP interpretation + causal inference); large-scale observational data from Airbnb; quasi-experimental design
- **Key findings:** AIGC effectiveness is heterogeneous by listing baseline performance; prompt design quality is the primary lever for marketing outcomes; underperforming brands/listings benefit most from AI content assistance.
- **Potential for our research:** Rare field-evidence paper using secondary/observational data (not lab experiment) — aligns with our priority. The "heterogeneous treatment effect by baseline brand equity" finding has direct implications for AI adoption ROI in branding contexts.
- **Relevance:** ★★★★★

### Beyond the Hype: Evaluating the Impact of Generative AI on Brand Authenticity, Image, and Consumer Behavior in the Restaurant Industry
- **Authors:** Laiba Ali, Faizan Ali, Moh'd Juma Abdalla, Salman Alotaibi
- **Venue:** International Journal of Hospitality Management | **Year:** 2025
- **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijhm.2025.002415
- **AI technology involved:** Generative AI (text-to-image and text generation for social media content)
- **Brand/marketing application:** Whether AI-generated social media content damages brand authenticity, brand image, and consumer behavioral intentions vs. human-created content
- **Abstract summary:** Uses a scenario-based experiment grounded in Stimulus-Organism-Response (S-O-R) theory to compare consumer responses to AI-generated vs. human-created restaurant social media posts. MANOVA results show perceived brand authenticity, image, and self-brand congruity are significantly lower in the GenAI condition. Multigroup analysis reveals that under GenAI conditions, self-brand congruity (not authenticity) becomes the primary driver of e-WOM and behavioral intentions.
- **Methodology:** Scenario-based experiment (S-O-R framework); MANOVA; multigroup structural equation modeling
- **Key findings:** GenAI social media content creates an authenticity penalty; however, consumers who already identify strongly with the brand (high self-brand congruity) still respond positively even to AI-generated content — brand equity acts as a buffer.
- **Potential for our research:** Directly applicable to questions about AIGC disclosure and brand authenticity management. The finding that brand–self congruity moderates the "GenAI penalty" suggests a promising boundary condition for future research.
- **Relevance:** ★★★★★

### Consumer Empowerment Through Generative Artificial Intelligence: Enhancing Brand Narrative with Collaboration, Creation, and Communication
- **Authors:** Yang Feng, Yuan Sun
- **Venue:** International Journal of Advertising, Vol. 45, No. 1 | **Year:** 2025
- **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1080/02650487.2025.2547545
- **AI technology involved:** Generative AI (ChatGPT-style tools for consumer co-creation)
- **Brand/marketing application:** How brands can use GenAI to invite consumers to co-create brand narratives and the effect on consumer empowerment, brand attitudes, and purchase intentions
- **Abstract summary:** Employs a mixed methods–grounded theory design (17 in-depth interviews + survey of 326 GenAI users) to identify three co-creation constructs: collaboration, creation, and communication. Collaboration and communication with GenAI tools enhance consumer empowerment (→ better brand attitudes and purchase intentions), while the "creation" dimension follows an inverted U-shaped curve with empowerment — too much creative control triggers cognitive overload rather than empowerment.
- **Methodology:** Mixed methods grounded theory (MM-GT); 17 semi-structured interviews; survey with 326 respondents; PLS-SEM
- **Key findings:** GenAI-facilitated co-creation can amplify brand narratives via consumer empowerment, but only when the consumer role is clearly structured; over-automation of creative tasks by AI undermines the empowerment effect.
- **Potential for our research:** Useful for designing AI co-creation brand campaigns. The inverted U-shaped relationship between AI creative autonomy and consumer empowerment is a nuanced finding with direct implications for "how much AI is too much" in brand strategy.
- **Relevance:** ★★★★☆
