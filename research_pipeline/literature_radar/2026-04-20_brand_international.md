### Research on The Internationalization Configuration Path of Chinese Brands: Considering Three Types of Globalizing Brands
- **Authors:** Junfeng Liao, Baojie Fu, Xinming He, Minru Yang
- **Journal:** SSRN Working Paper (abstract_id: 5819532) | **Year:** 2026
- **Geographic focus:** China → Global (goods, services, culture brand sectors)
- **Brand/industry:** Cross-sector — 172 Chinese brands spanning manufactured goods, service brands, and cultural brands
- **Abstract summary:** This paper investigates the heterogeneous internationalization pathways of Chinese brands across three industry categories: goods, services, and culture. It challenges traditional brand internationalization models by incorporating industry heterogeneity as a core explanatory variable. The study maps how Chinese brands are transforming from exporting goods to delivering services and cultural value on the global stage.
- **Methodology:** Mixed-methods — Natural Language Processing (NLP) for brand characteristic coding + Fuzzy-Set Qualitative Comparative Analysis (fsQCA) + Multiple Regression on 172 Chinese overseas brands. **Uses secondary/public data (brand data, online text corpora).**
- **Key findings:** Cross-cultural adaptability of brand personality profiles significantly determines overseas market recognition efficiency. Configurational combinations of technological innovation capability, market expansion ability, and relational embeddedness produce three distinct high-performance internationalization pathways depending on brand sector.
- **Research gap signaled:** Calls for longitudinal tracking of Chinese brand internationalization trajectories; future research on cultural distance moderation across the three brand-type pathways; dynamic capability reconfiguration as brands scale.
- **China relevance:** ★★★★★ Directly studies Chinese brand globalization (品牌出海) using a large empirical sample. Distinguishes goods/services/culture brand strategies — critical for understanding 华为, 茶颜悦色, 李宁 vs. export-OEM brands.
- **Relevance:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

### Future Trends Shaping International Marketing Research: A Horizon-Scanning Agenda for 2026–2030
- **Authors:** Not individually named in search results
- **Journal:** Journal of International Marketing and Marketing Research (JIMMR) | Vol. 21, Issue 3 | **Year:** 2026
- **Geographic focus:** Global / cross-regional
- **Brand/industry:** Cross-sector (AI, platform economy, sustainability, geopolitics)
- **Abstract summary:** International marketing research is entering a period of rapid reconfiguration driven by generative AI, tightening data and AI governance, platform-mediated market access, and intensifying sustainability and geopolitical constraints. The paper proposes a horizon-scanning agenda identifying the most consequential research priorities for 2026–2030.
- **Methodology:** Conceptual / horizon-scanning review. Not empirical.
- **Key findings:** Identifies five major forces reshaping international marketing: generative AI integration, AI/data regulation heterogeneity across markets, platform-mediated brand access (e.g., TikTok Shop, Amazon Global), sustainability-linked brand legitimacy demands, and geopolitical fragmentation of global markets.
- **Research gap signaled:** Calls for empirical work on AI-driven brand localization; cross-market regulatory compliance as a brand asset; brand resilience under deglobalization; platform-brand co-dependency governance.
- **China relevance:** ★★★★ Directly relevant — platforms like TikTok Shop, Temu, and Shein are cited as key sites for studying platform-mediated brand internationalization.
- **Relevance:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐

### The International Expansion of Digital Platforms: A Dynamic Setting for Challenging and Advancing Theories of the Multinational Enterprise
- **Authors:** Esteban García-Canal, Mauro F. Guillén
- **Journal:** Business Research Quarterly | **Year:** 2026
- **Geographic focus:** Global, with focus on platform-mediated markets (US, EU, Asia)
- **Brand/industry:** Digital platforms — broker vs. provider models (analogous to marketplace vs. own-brand e-commerce)
- **Abstract summary:** This conceptual paper examines how digital platforms internationalize through mechanisms distinct from traditional multinational enterprises. It focuses on competitive strategies, governance mechanisms, and non-market strategies, distinguishing broker platforms (facilitators) from provider platforms (resellers). The authors argue that platform internationalization creates new theoretical challenges for extant IB theories.
- **Methodology:** Conceptual / theoretical framework development.
- **Key findings:** Digital platforms leverage network effects to scale globally without proportional physical asset investment. Broker platforms internationalise with light infrastructure; provider platforms require substantial local investment and regulatory adaptation. Born-global digital firms can achieve rapid international scale through platform attachment rather than FDI.
- **Research gap signaled:** Empirical studies comparing platform vs. traditional MNE internationalization performance; how platform governance structures mediate brand trust across cultures; regulatory barriers as asymmetric impediments to platform globalization.
- **China relevance:** ★★★★ Directly applicable to Chinese platform brands: TikTok/ByteDance (broker), Temu/PDD Holdings (provider), AliExpress (hybrid). Framework explains strategic divergence in their global expansion.
- **Relevance:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐

### Global Marketing Research: Gains, Gaps, and Guardrails
- **Authors:** Weng Marc Lim et al. (multi-author including ICG affiliates)
- **Journal:** Journal of Global Marketing, Vol. 39, Issue 1 | **Year:** 2026 (Published online: January 22, 2026)
- **Geographic focus:** Global / meta-review of the field
- **Brand/industry:** Cross-sector (global brands across industries)
- **Abstract summary:** A deliberate critical narrative review of the global marketing research field, revisiting its signature debates: standardization vs. adaptation, global vs. local brands, culture as constraint vs. resource. The authors surface fragile assumptions in accepted wisdom and propose a forward-looking research agenda.
- **Methodology:** Narrative critical review / meta-analysis. Not empirical. Synthesizes decades of prior literature.
- **Key findings:** Many canonical findings in global marketing rest on unstated assumptions that may not travel across contexts. The standardization-adaptation dichotomy is increasingly inadequate; element-specific adaptation (adapting particular brand elements while standardizing others) is more predictive of success. The field has underweighted the role of digital platforms in disrupting classical international brand management.
- **Research gap signaled:** Need for multi-country longitudinal studies; greater granularity in what is standardized vs. adapted at the sub-brand level; understudied interaction of geopolitics and brand equity.
- **China relevance:** ★★★ Relevant indirectly — provides theoretical scaffolding for Chinese brand researchers challenging Western-centric standardization frameworks.
- **Relevance:** ⭐⭐⭐

### Empowering China's Cross-Border E-Commerce: High-Quality Development Explored Through fsQCA
- **Authors:** Not individually named in current search results
- **Journal:** Humanities and Social Sciences Communications (Palgrave/Nature), Vol. 12, Article 1183 | **Year:** 2025 (December)
- **Geographic focus:** China / Chinese CBEC firms exporting globally
- **Brand/industry:** Cross-border e-commerce (CBEC) — platform sellers and SME brand exporters
- **Abstract summary:** Examines drivers of high-quality development for Chinese cross-border e-commerce firms in a post-COVID environment characterized by rising logistics costs, fragmented regulatory environments, and intensifying platform competition. Using fsQCA on a sample of 95 CBEC firms, the study identifies necessary and sufficient configurations for high performance.
- **Methodology:** Fuzzy-Set Qualitative Comparative Analysis (fsQCA), n=95 Chinese CBEC firms. **Uses secondary/industry data (platform metrics, firm-level financial data).**
- **Key findings:** No single factor alone guarantees high-quality CBEC development. Three driving configurations emerge: (1) Market Expansion Driving path, (2) Core Competence–Policy Driving path, and (3) External Conditions Driving path. Market expansion capability, employee educational background, and government financial support are the most common core conditions.
- **Research gap signaled:** Longitudinal studies of CBEC brand equity building (not just sales performance); cross-platform comparative studies (Amazon vs. Shopee vs. TikTok Shop); cultural adaptation of product listings as brand signals.
- **China relevance:** ★★★★★ Directly examines Chinese brand exporters' high-performance configurations — actionable for understanding 品牌出海 vs. OEM-exit strategies. Secondary data methodology is replicable.
- **Relevance:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐

### Born Globals, International New Ventures, and International Entrepreneurship: Reflections and a Research Agenda
- **Authors:** Not individually named in current search results
- **Journal:** International Business Review | **Year:** 2025 (August)
- **Geographic focus:** Global / cross-regional comparative
- **Brand/industry:** Born-global SMEs and INVs across sectors (technology-intensive industries prominent)
- **Abstract summary:** A retrospective and prospective review marking the 30th and 20th anniversaries of the seminal Oviatt & McDougall (1994) and Knight & Cavusgil (2004) works. The paper synthesizes accumulated knowledge on born-global firms and international new ventures and identifies unresolved questions, emerging phenomena, and a future research agenda.
- **Methodology:** Systematic literature review / conceptual.
- **Key findings:** Born-global research has peaked (2018–2023 was the high-output period), coinciding with the rise of digital platforms. Digital-native born-global brands challenge the original asset-based internationalization frameworks. Social networks remain critical mediators of early internationalization performance.
- **Research gap signaled:** Born-digital vs. born-global distinction needs theorizing; platform-enabled internationalization of INVs from emerging markets; role of founder cultural identity in shaping brand positioning abroad.
- **China relevance:** ★★★ Relevant to Chinese digital-native brands (e.g., Shein, Anker, DJI) that exhibit born-global characteristics but defy Western INV frameworks.
- **Relevance:** ⭐⭐⭐
