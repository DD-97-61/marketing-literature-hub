### Global Marketing Research: Gains, Gaps, and Guardrails
- **Authors:** Weng Marc Lim, Preriit Souda, et al.
- **Journal:** Journal of Global Marketing | **Year:** 2026 (Vol. 39, Issue 1; published online January 22, 2026)
- **DOI:** 10.1080/08911762.2025.2610128
- **Geographic focus:** Global / cross-national (multi-market perspective)
- **Brand/industry:** Broad — covers all international/global marketing contexts
- **Abstract summary:** A deliberately critical narrative review that revisits global marketing research's foundational debates — standardization vs. adaptation, global vs. local brands, culture as constraint vs. resource — to expose fragile assumptions. The paper proposes a forward research agenda with explicit "guardrails" for future scholarship to avoid methodological and conceptual pitfalls.
- **Methodology:** Critical narrative review of extant global marketing literature (secondary/literature synthesis). Not empirical.
- **Key findings:** Accepted wisdom in global marketing (e.g., the standardization/adaptation dichotomy) is more fragile than commonly assumed; the field has underrepresented emerging market perspectives; future research needs more rigorous cross-cultural methods and context-sensitive theory.
- **Research gap signaled:** Emerging market brand dynamics; platform-mediated global marketing; AI's role in global brand strategy; decolonizing international marketing theory.
- **China relevance:** High — explicitly identifies emerging market perspectives (including China) as a critical gap in global marketing research; findings directly relevant to 品牌出海 scholarship.
- **Relevance:** ★★★★★

### Revising the Concept of a Perceived Brand Globalness: Beyond Broad Market Reach
- **Authors:** Robert Jadach, Agnieszka Tłuczak
- **Journal:** Journal of International Consumer Marketing | **Year:** 2025 (ahead-of-print, published May 2025)
- **DOI:** 10.1080/08961530.2025.2507647
- **Geographic focus:** International (Poland-based samples with cross-market implications)
- **Brand/industry:** General consumer brands (four product categories)
- **Abstract summary:** Challenges the traditional operationalization of Perceived Brand Globalness (PBG) — which relied mainly on "broad market reach" — by introducing "global brand symbolism" as a distinct and previously unmeasured dimension. Tests a revised conceptual model demonstrating that symbolism is a stronger driver of purchase intentions than reach alone.
- **Methodology:** Quantitative — path analysis and regression analysis on two samples: 194 university students and a 1,100-person representative sample across four product categories. **Uses primary survey data.**
- **Key findings:** Global brand symbolism positively and significantly influences consumer purchase intentions (β = 0.662), outperforming broad market reach (β = 0.550); perceived brand quality partially mediates both effects. Revises PBG construct with a two-dimensional model.
- **Research gap signaled:** Need to test revised PBG model across different cultural contexts (especially emerging markets); longitudinal designs to capture PBG shifts; application to digital-native brands.
- **China relevance:** Moderate — PBG/PBL framework widely applied in Chinese market studies (consumer nationalism, ethnocentrism); the new "symbolism" dimension is particularly relevant for Chinese brands seeking to signal global prestige without mere geographic breadth.
- **Relevance:** ★★★★

### Strategic Localization Practices and Glocalization Dynamics: Insights from the Fashion Industry
- **Authors:** Alice Noris, Nadzeya Sabatini, Lorenzo Cantoni
- **Journal:** Humanities and Social Sciences Communications (Nature/Palgrave) | **Year:** 2025
- **DOI:** 10.1057/s41599-025-05542-z
- **Geographic focus:** Global fashion markets (eCommerce and social media platforms across multiple regions)
- **Brand/industry:** Fashion and apparel brands (luxury and mass market)
- **Abstract summary:** An inductive qualitative study of how fashion brands operationalize glocalization — maintaining global brand coherence while implementing culturally specific adaptations on eCommerce and social media. Identifies three core localization dimensions and argues that localization has become a strategic imperative, not merely a tactical adjustment.
- **Methodology:** Qualitative — 17 semi-structured interviews with fashion industry executives; grounded theory approach. **Uses primary qualitative data.**
- **Key findings:** Three core localization dimensions emerged: (1) *Spatial localization* (language, payment systems, visual identity); (2) *Temporal alignment* (seasonality, cultural calendar synchronization); (3) *Culture-driven consumer adaptation* (product offerings, size ranges, aesthetics). Inclusivity and cultural sensitivity are now central to global brand management, not peripheral.
- **Research gap signaled:** Impact of generative AI on localization decisions; platform-specific glocalization strategies in emerging markets (Middle East, Southeast Asia); quantitative validation of the three-dimension framework.
- **China relevance:** Moderate-High — directly applicable to Chinese fashion brands (e.g., Shein, ANTA) navigating cross-border eCommerce localization; social media platform localization findings relevant for brands adapting to TikTok/Instagram vs. WeChat/Xiaohongshu contexts.
- **Relevance:** ★★★★

### Research on The Internationalization Configuration Path of Chinese Brands: Considering Three Types of Globalizing Brands ⭐ CHINA HIGH PRIORITY
- **Authors:** Junfeng Liao, Baojie Fu, Xinming He, Minru Yang
- **Journal:** SSRN Working Paper (International Business eJournal) | **Year:** 2025 (submitted November 2025)
- **SSRN Abstract ID:** 5819532
- **Geographic focus:** Chinese brands expanding globally (goods, services, and cultural brands)
- **Brand/industry:** Multiple sectors — consumer goods, services, cultural/creative industries
- **Abstract summary:** Challenges traditional one-size-fits-all internationalization models by introducing industry heterogeneity — arguing that goods brands, services brands, and cultural brands follow fundamentally different overseas configuration paths. Uses a multi-method approach combining NLP, fsQCA, and regression to analyze 172 Chinese brands, mapping distinct pathways for each type.
- **Methodology:** Multi-method — Natural Language Processing (NLP) for text analysis of brand reports, fuzzy set Qualitative Comparative Analysis (fsQCA), and multiple regression. **Uses secondary/public data on 172 Chinese overseas brands.** Flagged: secondary data.
- **Key findings:** Goods brands, services brands, and cultural brands each require distinct internationalization configurations; traditional internationalization theory (OLI, Uppsala) inadequately accounts for Chinese cultural brands going global; institutional and relational factors interact differently across brand types.
- **Research gap signaled:** Dynamic reconfiguration of paths over time; brand equity building in host markets for services vs. cultural brands; role of digital platforms in enabling cultural brand internationalization.
- **China relevance:** ★ VERY HIGH — directly maps Chinese brand internationalization paths across goods/services/culture; most directly applicable to 品牌出海 research agenda. Uses public data, making replication feasible.
- **Relevance:** ★★★★★

### Culturally Mixed Co-Branding Product Framing in China: The Role of Cultural Sensitivity, Product Quality, and Purchase Probability ⭐ CHINA HIGH PRIORITY
- **Authors:** [Multiple authors — full list at DOI]
- **Journal:** Humanities and Social Sciences Communications (Nature/Palgrave) | **Year:** 2024
- **DOI:** 10.1057/s41599-024-02954-1
- **Geographic focus:** China (Chinese consumer market)
- **Brand/industry:** Consumer goods (foreign brands × patriotic/local brands in cross-border co-branding)
- **Abstract summary:** Examines two framing strategies for culturally mixed co-branded products (CMCPs) in China: Foreign Brand × Patriotic Brand (FB × PB) and Patriotic Brand × Foreign Brand (PB × FB). Conducted amid a rising patriotism wave among Chinese consumers, the study reveals asymmetric effects depending on whether the foreign or local brand leads the co-brand framing.
- **Methodology:** Experimental design — manipulated co-branding product frames (FB × PB vs. PB × FB) with Chinese consumer participants; measured cultural sensitivity, product quality perception, and purchase probability. **Uses primary experimental data.**
- **Key findings:** PB × FB framing generates stronger cultural congruence effects and co-branding attitude through cultural fit; FB × PB framing generates stronger brand fit effects. The influence of co-branding attitude on cultural sensitivity was significant only for PB × FB, while the influence on product quality was significant only for FB × PB — confirming asymmetric framing effects.
- **Research gap signaled:** Longitudinal effects of patriotism on co-branding receptivity; three-way co-branding across cultures; social media context effects on culturally mixed framing.
- **China relevance:** ★ VERY HIGH — directly addresses Chinese consumer behavior toward cross-border co-branding; critical for international brands entering China and for Chinese brands co-branding with foreign partners in outbound markets.
- **Relevance:** ★★★★★

### New Internationalization Paths of Chinese Brands: A Configurational Study ⭐ CHINA HIGH PRIORITY
- **Authors:** Liao, Yang et al. [full author list at DOI — note: different paper from SSRN Liao et al. 2025 above]
- **Journal:** PLOS ONE | **Year:** 2023 (published September 29, 2023)
- **DOI:** 10.1371/journal.pone.0292253
- **Note:** Included despite being outside the 14-day search window due to foundational relevance to 品牌出海 research and high citation potential.
- **Geographic focus:** Chinese brands in global markets (Huawei, Xiaomi, Transsion as key cases)
- **Brand/industry:** Technology, consumer electronics, telecom
- **Abstract summary:** Using fsQCA on 61 representative Chinese brands, explores joint influences of internal capabilities (technological innovation, IP protection) and external factors (market expansion, business environment, relational embeddedness, cultural distance) on brand internationalization. Identifies two distinct modes absent in Western brand internationalization theory.
- **Methodology:** fsQCA (fuzzy set Qualitative Comparative Analysis) on 61 Chinese brands. **Uses secondary/public data on Chinese brand performance and characteristics.** Flagged: secondary data.
- **Key findings:** Two uniquely Chinese internationalization modes identified: (1) *Progressive mode* — similar to developed-economy brands (e.g., Huawei's "L-M-Co" path); (2) *Leapfrog mode* — bypassing traditional stages via digital platforms and relational networks (e.g., Xiaomi, Transsion). Four distinct configurational paths mapped in total.
- **Research gap signaled:** Dynamic evolution across modes over time; applicability to non-tech sectors (beauty, apparel, food); role of government relations in path selection.
- **China relevance:** ★ VERY HIGH — foundational configurational study of Chinese brand internationalization; directly relevant to understanding how 品牌出海 strategies differ from Western MNC playbooks.
- **Relevance:** ★★★★★
