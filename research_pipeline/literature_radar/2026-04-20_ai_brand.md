### In Agents We Trust, but Who Do Agents Trust? Latent Source Preferences Steer LLM Generations
- **Authors:** Mohammad Aflah Khan, Mahsa Amani, Soumi Das, Bishwamittra Ghosh, Qinyuan Wu, Krishna P. Gummadi, Manish Gupta, Abhilasha Ravichander
- **Venue:** ICLR 2026 (Conference Paper) | **Year:** 2026
- **Preprint:** arXiv:2602.15456 (submitted February 2026)
- **AI technology involved:** LLMs / AI information agents
- **Brand/marketing application:** Reveals that LLMs encode latent brand preferences that systematically bias which information they surface when acting as shopping or recommendation agents — directly threatening brand equity in AI-mediated discovery.
- **Abstract summary:** LLM-based agents increasingly act as information gatekeepers on online platforms, filtering and synthesizing product/service information for users. This paper demonstrates that LLMs encode implicit latent preferences for particular brands and sources, and that these hidden biases steer the information prioritized in agent outputs. The findings expose a structural asymmetry favoring well-established brands in LLM recommendations.
- **Methodology:** Controlled experiments querying multiple LLMs across information-retrieval and recommendation tasks; systematic analysis of brand-preference signatures in model outputs.
- **Key findings:** LLMs exhibit measurable, consistent latent source preferences that are not disclosed to users; established brands receive disproportionate positive prioritization compared to newer competitors.
- **Potential for our research:** Foundational for a research agenda on "AI brand equity" — how much of a brand's future visibility depends on LLM training data rather than actual consumer preference. Could inspire field studies measuring brand mention frequency across LLM outputs over time.
- **Relevance:** ★★★★★

### Large Language Models for Market Research: A Data-Augmentation Approach
- **Authors:** Mengxin Wang, Dennis J. Zhang, Heng Zhang
- **Venue:** Marketing Science | **Year:** 2026
- **DOI:** 10.1287/mksc.2025.0009
- **Preprint:** arXiv:2412.19363
- **AI technology involved:** LLMs (transfer learning / data augmentation)
- **Brand/marketing application:** Methodology for efficiently integrating LLM-simulated survey respondents with real human data in conjoint analysis — enabling lower-cost brand preference and product positioning research.
- **Abstract summary:** Proposes a novel statistical data-augmentation framework that combines LLM-generated synthetic respondent data with small real-human samples in conjoint studies. By using transfer learning principles to debias LLM outputs against human anchors, the method achieves near-full-sample accuracy at dramatically reduced data-collection cost. The approach is validated on brand preference and feature valuation tasks.
- **Methodology:** Conjoint analysis with LLM data augmentation; transfer learning debiasing; comparison against human-only benchmarks using held-out validation.
- **Key findings:** LLM-augmented samples match or closely approximate human conjoint results when debiased with a small real sample; the approach reduces research costs substantially without sacrificing validity.
- **Potential for our research:** High direct applicability — enables rapid, lower-cost brand equity measurement and ad testing. Especially useful for studying brand perceptions across demographic subgroups where recruiting participants is difficult.
- **Relevance:** ★★★★★

- **Venue:** Global Business Review (SAGE) | **Year:** 2026 (Published online: January 20, 2026)

- **AI technology involved:** Agentic AI (autonomous decision-making systems)
- **Brand/marketing application:** Examines how autonomous AI agents — capable of independent decision-making, learning, and adaptation — transform passive consumer–brand interactions into dynamic, co-creative relationships and redefine brand management processes.
- **Abstract summary:** This conceptual-empirical paper explores how agentic AI redefines branding by transforming consumer touchpoints from scripted interactions to adaptive, real-time co-creation. ### Brands Cannot Own a Human Celebrity but Can Own an AI Endorser: AI Endorsers' Role in Shaping Consumer Self-Brand Connection
- **Authors:** Xinran Wang, Huanzhang Wang
- **Venue:** Journal of Retailing and Consumer Services, Vol. 90 | **Year:** 2026 (March 2026)
- **DOI:** 10.1016/j.jretconser.2025.104717
- **AI technology involved:** AI-generated virtual brand endorsers / CGI spokespersons
- **Brand/marketing application:** Investigates whether brand-created (proprietary) AI endorsers generate stronger consumer self-brand connection than public (shared) AI endorsers, and identifies the mechanisms involved.
- **Abstract summary:** As AI-generated endorsers enter mainstream advertising, brands face a strategic choice: create a proprietary AI spokesperson vs. co-opt a publicly available AI persona. This paper demonstrates, through multiple experiments, that brand-created AI endorsers produce stronger consumer self-brand connection than public AI endorsers. The effect is serially mediated by perceived endorser–brand match and parasocial interaction.
- **Methodology:** Pre-test plus three main experiments (online between-subjects designs); moderated serial mediation analysis; stimuli featuring real and fictitious brand–endorser pairings.
- **Key findings:** Brand-created AI endorsers outperform public AI endorsers on self-brand connection via higher perceived fit and parasocial bond; the endorser-ownership boundary creates a meaningful competitive advantage for brands.
- **Potential for our research:** Raises novel questions about brand asset strategy in the AI era — ownership of AI endorsers as brand IP. Could be extended using field data from brands that have launched proprietary vs. shared AI spokespersons.
- **Relevance:** ★★★★★

### AGENTIC COMMERCE: A Theory of Markets When the Shopper Is No Longer Human
- **Authors:** Paul F. Accornero
- **Venue:** SSRN Working Paper | **Year:** 2026 (Published: January 2, 2026)
- **SSRN:** abstract_id=6111766
- **AI technology involved:** Autonomous AI purchasing agents (agentic AI)
- **Brand/marketing application:** Develops a comprehensive theoretical architecture for how brand marketing must evolve when AI agents — not humans — execute search, evaluation, and purchase decisions on behalf of consumers.
- **Abstract summary:** This 70-page working paper defines "agentic commerce" as commercial exchange where AI systems execute purchasing on delegated human authority, projects a $3–5 trillion market impact by 2030, and synthesizes principal-agent theory, information economics, and platform economics into a unified framework. Thirteen falsifiable propositions are derived and supported by computational simulation.
- **Methodology:** Theoretical framework construction (principal-agent theory + information economics + platform economics); computational simulation; deductive proposition development.
- **Key findings:** AI agents as shoppers create information asymmetries and goal-misalignment problems (moral hazard) between consumers and their purchasing agents; traditional brand persuasion tools targeting human psychology become ineffective when the "shopper" is an algorithm optimizing on explicit criteria.
- **Potential for our research:** Paradigm-shifting for brand strategy theory — if brand choice is increasingly delegated to AI agents, what are the new levers of brand influence? Excellent theoretical foundation for empirical work on "algorithmic brand selection."
- **Relevance:** ★★★★

### The Power of AI-Generated Content: Evidence From the Peer-to-Peer Accommodation Market
- **Authors:** Ningyuan Fan, Xiang (Robert) Li, Chao Liu, Zhi-Ping Fan
- **Venue:** Journal of Travel Research | **Year:** 2026
- **DOI:** 10.1177/00472875251332951
- **AI technology involved:** AIGC / NLP (AI as language assistant and content creator)
- **Brand/marketing application:** Quantifies how AI-generated listing descriptions affect real marketplace performance (bookings, revenue) on Airbnb — an observational test of AIGC branding in practice.
- **Abstract summary:** Using a prediction-interpretation-inference machine learning framework applied to Airbnb listing data, this study evaluates AIGC efficacy under two conditions: AI as a language assistant (polishing human-written text) vs. AI as a content creator (generating descriptions from scratch). Results reveal that prompt design strategy significantly moderates AIGC effectiveness, and that AIGC has heterogeneous effects by baseline listing performance.
- **Methodology:** Observational field data from Airbnb platform; prediction-interpretation-inference ML pipeline; causal inference via difference-in-differences; heterogeneous treatment effect analysis.
- **Key findings:** AIGC boosts bookings and revenue for underperforming listings; for high-performing listings, AIGC can be counterproductive; AI performs better as language assistant than as standalone content creator.
- **Potential for our research:** Rare use of observational marketplace data to causally estimate AIGC effects — a strong methodological template. Could be replicated across other categories (e.g., e-commerce product pages) to generalize AIGC branding effects.

- **AI technology involved:** AI-generated content / AIGC disclosure labeling
- **Brand/marketing application:** Tests whether mandatory "AI-generated" labels — increasingly required by regulators — meaningfully change consumer trust, credibility judgments, and sharing behavior for branded content on social media.
- **Abstract summary:** A two-wave longitudinal experiment (N=400 Wave 1; N=256 Wave 2; 18-day interval) examines cognitive and emotional responses to AIGC labels on social media posts. - **Potential for our research:** Directly relevant to brand communications strategy under AI-disclosure regulations. The "emotional tax" finding has implications for ad effectiveness metrics when AIGC content is labeled. Could extend to brand-specific stimuli.

### The Impact of Generative AI on Social Media: An Experimental Study
- **Authors:** Anders Giovanni Møller, Daniel M. Romero, David Jurgens, Luca Maria Aiello
- **Venue:** Scientific Reports (Nature Portfolio) | **Year:** 2026
- **DOI:** 10.1038/s41598-026-40110-8
- **Preprint:** arXiv:2506.14295
- **AI technology involved:** Generative AI (conversation assistance, reply suggestion, feedback tools)
- **Brand/marketing application:** Evaluates how AI writing assistance tools embedded in social media affect content quality, perceived authenticity, and engagement — critical concerns for brands using AI in community management.
- **Abstract summary:** A controlled experiment with 680 U.S. participants in simulated social media discussion groups tested four AI intervention conditions (Chat assistance, Conversation Starters, Comment Feedback, Reply Suggestions) against a control. While some AI tools increased content volume and engagement, they simultaneously decreased perceived quality and authenticity, with negative spillover effects on non-AI-assisted conversations in the same thread.
- **Methodology:** Large-scale controlled online experiment (N=680); between-subjects factorial design; measurement of engagement, perceived quality, and authenticity; spillover analysis.
- **Key findings:** AI assistance tools increase engagement volume but degrade perceived authenticity — a quality–quantity trade-off; AI-generated content creates negative authenticity spillovers that degrade surrounding human-generated content in the same thread.
- **Potential for our research:** The authenticity spillover finding is novel and under-studied — brands using AI for community management may inadvertently degrade the perceived authenticity of their entire brand conversation thread. Strong candidate for a brand-specific replication.
- **Relevance:** ★★★
