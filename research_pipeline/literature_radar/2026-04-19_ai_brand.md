### Agentic AI in Branding: Driving Hyper-personalization and Real-time Adaptation

- **Venue:** Global Business Review (SAGE) | **Year:** 2026
- **DOI:** 10.1177/09721509251414773
- **AI technology involved:** Agentic AI — autonomous LLM-based systems capable of independent decision-making and adaptation
- **Brand/marketing application:** Redefining brand personalization strategy; co-creative consumer–brand relationships enabled by AI agents that sense, decide, and act in real time
- **Abstract summary:** The paper argues that agentic AI transforms branding from passive content delivery to dynamic, self-directed consumer interaction. Drawing on self-determination theory (SDT), the authors theorise that agentic AI can satisfy consumers' psychological needs for autonomy, competence, and relatedness, deepening brand engagement. The paper offers practitioners a governance framework for deploying agentic AI without eroding brand integrity.
- **Methodology:** Conceptual/theoretical framework paper; synthesises SDT and branding literature with illustrative industry cases
- **Key findings:** Agentic AI autonomy enhances intrinsic motivation and brand co-creation when governed with human-oversight checkpoints; misaligned autonomy risks brand equity erosion.
- **Potential for our research:** First formal academic treatment of *agentic* AI (vs. generative AI) as a branding tool — opens questions about where the boundary of brand voice control lies and how brands should design governance policies for autonomous AI agents.
- **Relevance:** ★★★★★

### The Power of AI-Generated Content: Evidence From the Peer-to-Peer Accommodation Market
- **Authors:** Ningyuan Fan, Xiang (Robert) Li, Chao Liu, Zhi-Ping Fan
- **Venue:** Journal of Travel Research (SAGE) | **Year:** 2026
- **DOI:** 10.1177/00472875251332951
- **AI technology involved:** AI-generated content (AIGC) — LLM-based text generation for host listings
- **Brand/marketing application:** Platform brand management; how host-level AIGC adoption shapes listing performance and platform ecosystem
- **Abstract summary:** Using large-scale observational data from a P2P accommodation platform, the study identifies the causal effect of AIGC adoption on listing-level outcomes. The design exploits variation in host AIGC uptake to isolate platform performance effects. Findings reveal asymmetric benefits: AIGC improves descriptions and booking rates for inexperienced hosts but can signal inauthenticity for high-reputation hosts.
- **Methodology:** Quasi-experimental design with secondary/observational data from a P2P platform; difference-in-differences estimation
- **Key findings:** AIGC adoption boosts conversion for low-equity hosts; diminishing or negative returns for established hosts with strong brand equity, highlighting an authenticity penalty.
- **Potential for our research:** Strong secondary-data design (rare in AIGC literature). The authenticity penalty finding has direct implications for brand managers deciding how much AI content to disclose or integrate across brand tiers.
- **Relevance:** ★★★★★

### How Users Perceive and React to Labeled AI-Generated Content on Social Media: A Longitudinal Study of Cognitive and Emotional Effects
- **Authors:** Li F., Liu Y., Yang Y., Yu G.
- **Venue:** International Journal of Human–Computer Interaction (Taylor & Francis) | **Year:** 2026
- **DOI:** 10.1080/10447318.2026.2618553
- **AI technology involved:** LLM-generated content (social media posts); AI disclosure labels
- **Brand/marketing application:** AI disclosure regulation; branded content credibility; advertiser trust management on social platforms
- **Abstract summary:** A two-wave longitudinal online experiment (Wave 1 N=400; Wave 2 N=256; 18-day interval) examined how AI-label exposure affects perceived accuracy, information credibility, and sharing intention over time. Posts varied by veracity (true vs. false) and content type (nonprofit news vs. commercial advertising). Self-control was tested as a moderating psychological trait.
- **Methodology:** Two-wave longitudinal online experiment with between-subjects design; cross-lagged panel modeling
- **Key findings:** Perceived accuracy, credibility, and sharing intention were stable across waves, suggesting AI labels have attenuated effects over time. Self-control moderates initial label effects but not their persistence.
- **Potential for our research:** Longitudinal design is methodologically rare; the stability finding challenges the assumption that AI disclosure policies will have durable effects on consumer trust — directly relevant for brand transparency strategy.
- **Relevance:** ★★★★

- **Venue:** SSRN Working Paper | **Year:** July 2025
- **SSRN:** abstract_id=5367123
- **AI technology involved:** LLMs / ChatGPT for marketing content generation
- **Brand/marketing application:** Brand differentiation, content uniqueness on social media, competitive positioning for SMEs
- **Abstract summary:** The paper exploits Italy's temporary ChatGPT ban as a natural experiment to isolate the causal effect of LLM adoption on Instagram marketing content homogenization among independent restaurants. Treated restaurants showed marked convergence in lexical, syntactic, semantic, and style dimensions, with a simultaneous drop in consumer engagement.

## Cross-cutting Themes This Week

1. **Content homogenization as a brand risk** — Two independent working papers (Liu et al.; Alavi & Nozari) using different platforms (Instagram, Amazon) and different AI tools converge on the same finding: LLM adoption reduces content diversity and consumer engagement. This is emerging as a core empirical regularity.

2. **AI disclosure penalty** — Lee et al.'s field experiment finds a 31.5% reduction in ad effectiveness upon disclosure; Li et al.'s longitudinal study shows labelling effects are unstable over time. Regulators pushing mandatory AI labelling may inadvertently penalise brands using AI for legitimate creative production.

3. **Agentic vs. generative AI for branding** — Puri et al. formally introduce the agentic AI / branding nexus. Combined with Hardcastle et al.'s autonomy-loss findings, this raises urgent questions about how much brand AI autonomy consumers will accept before trust erodes.

4. **Virtual influencers going autonomous** — Both the Audrezet et al. (JBR) and Kumar (IJCS) reviews independently flag fully autonomous AI influencers as the next frontier — yet empirical work is essentially absent. High-value research gap.
