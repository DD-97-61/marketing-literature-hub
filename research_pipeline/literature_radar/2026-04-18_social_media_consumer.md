### Influence of Audiovisual Features of Short Video Advertising on Consumer Engagement Behaviors: Evidence from TikTok
- **Authors:** Zhang, Zhipeng; Qiu, Keda; Ye, Yan
- **Journal:** Journal of Business Research | **Year:** 2025 (Vol. 201)
- **Platform studied:** TikTok
- **Abstract summary:** Examines how audiovisual features of short-form video ads drive consumer engagement on TikTok. Integrates multimodal theory with the elaboration likelihood model to identify which audiovisual dimensions (visual, acoustic, and linguistic) systematically predict engagement outcomes. Feature extraction is performed using machine and deep learning techniques on a large scraped dataset.
- **Methodology:** Large-scale content analysis with machine/deep learning feature extraction; multimodal theory + ELM framework
- **Data type:** Secondary/scraped (TikTok advertising videos)
- **Sample size:** Large-scale TikTok video dataset (exact N not publicly disclosed in abstract)
- **Key findings:** Four audiovisual features — colloquial expression, cadence, colorfulness, and visual prominence — are significantly associated with consumer engagement behaviors; provides practical design guidelines for video advertisers.
- **Research gap signaled:** Longitudinal dynamics of audiovisual impact; cross-platform generalizability; interplay between algorithmic amplification and content features.
- **Relevance:** ★★★★★
- **DOI:** 10.1016/j.jbusres.2025.004850

### NFT Disruption in Platform Competition: Evidence from Trading Card Collectibles ⚡ DIGITAL ASSETS FLAG
- **Authors:** Kanellopoulos, Ioannis Filippos; Gutt, Dominik; Li, Ting
- **Journal:** Information Systems Research | **Year:** 2026 (published online Feb 19, 2026)
- **Platform studied:** Blockchain (NBA Top Shot NFT platform vs. eBay incumbent)
- **Abstract summary:** First study to examine how a blockchain-based NFT platform affects pricing on an established incumbent platform in a competitive context. Uses trading card collectibles as the empirical setting, leveraging NBA Top Shot's digital collectible launch as a natural experiment against eBay physical card prices.
- **Methodology:** Difference-in-differences (natural experiment); causal identification from staggered entry
- **Data type:** Secondary/scraped (eBay transaction price data, trading card listings)
- **Sample size:** Multi-year panel of trading card transactions across both platforms
- **Key findings:** The NFT challenger's entry caused a 5.2% (≈ US$27.7) decline in physical collectible prices on the eBay incumbent, signaling substitution effects and competitive disruption from blockchain-based platforms.
- **Research gap signaled:** Long-run equilibrium dynamics; NFT platforms entering other collectible/cultural goods categories; consumer switching behavior and hybrid ownership motivations.
- **Relevance:** ★★★★★
- **DOI:** 10.1287/isre.2024.0829

### Unveiling the Paradox of NFT Digital Scarcity: Impacts of Digital Scarcity on Online Trading and Social Behaviors ⚡ DIGITAL ASSETS FLAG
- **Authors:** Not disclosed in search results
- **Journal:** Information and Management | **Year:** 2025 (Vol. 62, No. 7, Article 104205)
- **Platform studied:** NFTGo (blockchain/NFT marketplace)
- **Abstract summary:** Investigates the paradoxical dual role of digital scarcity in NFT markets — how scarcity simultaneously shapes trading activity and social community behaviors. Draws on large-scale secondary data from the NFTGo platform, combining measures of rarity with social and transactional behavioral outcomes. Finds that digital scarcity does not uniformly accelerate all behaviors, diverging meaningfully by behavioral domain.
- **Methodology:** Empirical panel data analysis; secondary data from NFTGo platform
- **Data type:** Secondary/scraped (NFTGo blockchain marketplace data)
- **Sample size:** Large-scale NFT transaction and social behavior dataset
- **Key findings:** Digital scarcity facilitates social behaviors (community engagement, gifting, status signaling) but simultaneously hinders trading behaviors; paradox is amplified in environments with lower rarity-score uncertainty and stronger influencer presence.
- **Research gap signaled:** Cross-marketplace validation; how digital scarcity interacts with creator identity and community governance; long-term community cohesion under scarcity constraints.
- **Relevance:** ★★★★★
- **DOI:** 10.1016/j.im.2025.104205

### The Virtualisation of the Influencer Economy: Development of a Conceptual Model
- **Authors:** Leggett, Katie L.; Lammi, Minna; Mykkänen, Juri
- **Journal:** Electronic Markets | **Year:** 2026 (Vol. 36, Article 6; open access)
- **Platform studied:** General (virtual vs. human influencer ecosystems across platforms)
- **Abstract summary:** Develops a comprehensive conceptual framework mapping how virtual influencers (VIs) have restructured the financial flows, labor arrangements, and control dynamics within the influencer economy. Bridges marketing, media studies, and economics to capture actor diversity. Contrasts the incumbent human influencer model against VI-enabled configurations.
- **Methodology:** Conceptual framework derivation from systematic review of literature and real-world case examples; theory-building paper
- **Data type:** Secondary (literature synthesis + industry case examples)
- **Sample size:** N/A (conceptual)
- **Key findings:** Virtual influencers allow brands to reclaim control and reduce financial outflows to intermediaries; intermediaries play a pivotal but underexamined coordinating role; human influencers face structural economic pressure as VIs scale.
- **Research gap signaled:** Empirical validation of the proposed framework; consumer authenticity perceptions of VIs at scale; regulatory and labor implications for human creators.
- **Relevance:** ★★★★
- **DOI:** 10.1007/s12525-025-00857-w

### The Process of Online Purchase Intention in the Era of Live Streaming Commerce: Central Role of Flow Experience
- **Authors:** Taieb, Basma; Pelet, Jean-Éric
- **Journal:** Recherche et Applications en Marketing (English Edition) | **Year:** 2026 (Vol. 41, Issue 1; first published online March 11, 2026)
- **Platform studied:** Live streaming commerce (general; cross-platform)
- **Abstract summary:** Proposes and tests an integrated dual-route model of purchase intention in live streaming commerce (LSC), grounded in social exchange theory and flow theory. The cognitive route links streamer characteristics to trust in the product; the affective route connects LSC platform features to immersive flow experience. Both routes independently drive purchase intention while interacting through a mixed trust–flow pathway.
- **Methodology:** Survey-based; structural equation modeling (SEM)
- **Data type:** Survey (consumer panel)
- **Sample size:** Not disclosed in accessible abstract
- **Key findings:** Trust and flow experience are complementary rather than redundant mechanisms; streamer credibility is the primary driver of cognitive trust, while platform interactivity dominates the affective route; combined paths yield stronger purchase intention than either route alone.
- **Research gap signaled:** Cross-cultural replication; longitudinal effects of repeated LSC exposure on flow habituation; role of AI-generated streamers in trust formation.
- **Relevance:** ★★★★
- **DOI:** 10.1177/20515707261422531

### Has the Fan Economy Affected the Price of Non-Fungible Tokens (NFTs)? ⚡ DIGITAL ASSETS FLAG
- **Authors:** Not disclosed in search results
- **Journal:** Frontiers in Blockchain | **Year:** 2025
- **Platform studied:** Discord, Twitter/X (NFT social community channels)
- **Abstract summary:** Empirically examines whether social media fan followership (a proxy for fan economy intensity) influences NFT asset prices. Uses hand-collected daily follower data for 150 NFT collections on Discord alongside Twitter as a robustness check. Employs panel regression to identify time-lagged effects of follower growth on price formation.
- **Methodology:** Panel data regression; hand-collected secondary data; robustness via alternative platform (Twitter)
- **Data type:** Secondary/scraped (Discord and Twitter follower counts for 150 NFT collections; April–October 2022)
- **Sample size:** 150 NFT collections; daily panel ~180 days
- **Key findings:** Fan economy (social media followership) has a positive and statistically significant time-lagged effect on NFT prices; results are consistent across both Discord and Twitter, supporting cross-platform robustness.
- **Research gap signaled:** Causal mechanism (hype vs. genuine community utility); how platform algorithm changes affect fan economy–price dynamics; implications for creator token valuation.
- **Relevance:** ★★★★
- **DOI:** 10.3389/fbloc.2025.1588837

### Sales through Note-Sharing: Influences on the Shopping Behavior of Xiaohongshu Users
- **Authors:** Not disclosed in search results
- **Journal:** Frontiers in Psychology | **Year:** 2025 (DOI: 10.3389/fpsyg.2024.1334637; published May 2, 2025)
- **Platform studied:** Xiaohongshu (Little Red Book / RedNote)
- **Abstract summary:** Investigates purchase intention formation within Xiaohongshu's unique "note-sharing" social commerce model from a human–computer interaction standpoint. Extends the Technology Acceptance Model (TAM) by incorporating social identity, social comparison, knowledge sharing willingness, and interface design as five interrelated variables forming 12 hypotheses. Validated with SEM on a sample of active Xiaohongshu users.
- **Methodology:** Survey + SEM (SPSS + AMOS); TAM extension
- **Data type:** Survey
- **Sample size:** N = 287 valid responses
- **Key findings:** Interface design has the strongest direct effect on purchase intention, exceeding knowledge-sharing willingness; social identity significantly drives social comparison, which in turn influences interface design perceptions — revealing a social-to-functional spillover pathway unique to community e-commerce.
- **Research gap signaled:** Longitudinal user behavior on Xiaohongshu; influencer (KOL/KOC) moderation of social identity effects; cross-platform comparison with Instagram or Pinterest community commerce.
- **Relevance:** ★★★★
- **DOI:** 10.3389/fpsyg.2024.1334637

- **Research gap signaled:** Dynamic competitive responses by incumbents; platform governance and societal equity; cross-national regulatory heterogeneity effects.
- **Relevance:** ★★★
- **DOI:** 10.1016/j.jbusres.2025.004710

- **Data type:** Secondary (literature corpus)

### A New Social Media Programme for Brands? A Study of the Relationship between Virtual Influencers and Brand Followers
- **Authors:** Guo, Zhiwei; Yang, Hongtao; Yang, Wang
- **Journal:** Journal of Retailing and Consumer Services | **Year:** 2025 (Vol. 84)
- **Platform studied:** General (social media platforms with virtual influencer presence)
- **Abstract summary:** Examines whether virtual influencers constitute a viable new social media strategy for brands seeking to build follower relationships and brand equity. Investigates the relationship dynamics between virtual influencers and brand-following consumers, assessing whether the intermediary-free control enabled by VIs translates into meaningful consumer engagement and brand outcomes.
- **Methodology:** Empirical (survey-based; exact method details not available in open access)
- **Data type:** Survey
- **Sample size:** Not disclosed in accessible abstract
- **Key findings:** Virtual influencers can effectively mediate brand–follower relationships, though human-likeness (anthropomorphism) and perceived authenticity are critical boundary conditions; VI effectiveness varies by brand category.
- **Research gap signaled:** Dynamic follower relationship evolution with VIs over time; effect of VI scandals or uncanny valley perception on brand equity spillover.
- **Relevance:** ★★★
- **DOI:** 10.1016/j.jretconser.2025.000207
