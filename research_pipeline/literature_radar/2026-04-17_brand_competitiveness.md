### The Resilient Grass in the Storm: Brand Resilience in Uncertain Contexts
- **Authors:** Li, C.; Hao, R.
- **Journal:** Journal of Brand Management | **Year:** 2026
- **DOI:** 10.1057/s41262-026-00432-4
- **Focus:** Brand resilience measurement / scale development
- **Abstract summary:** Theoretical research on brand resilience has remained underdeveloped, with conceptual ambiguity and lack of validated measurement tools hindering progress. This study addresses the gap by qualitatively identifying the conceptual dimensions of brand resilience through grounded theory and in-depth interviews, then constructing and validating a quantitative measurement scale. The result is a 10-item brand resilience scale clarifying the construct's structural boundaries.
- **Methodology:** Mixed methods — qualitative phase (grounded theory, in-depth interviews) to identify dimensions; quantitative phase (EFA + CFA) to develop and validate scale
- **Key findings:** Brand resilience comprises three dimensions — brand anticipation, brand adaptability, and brand transcendence. A 10-item scale was validated across samples.
- **Research gap signaled:** Application of the scale to longitudinal studies tracking brand resilience across economic downturns or supply chain shocks; cross-industry and cross-cultural validity of the three-factor model.
- **Data source used:** Primary data — interview transcripts + survey responses
- **Relevance:** ★★★★★

### How Can Businesses Enhance Customer Resistance to Brand Switching?
- **Authors:** Liu, Y.; Duffek, B.; Eisingerich, A. B.
- **Journal:** Journal of the Academy of Marketing Science (JAMS) | **Year:** 2026
- **Volume/Pages:** Vol. 54, pp. 207–227
- **DOI:** 10.1007/s11747-025-01115-3
- **Focus:** Brand defense / brand loyalty drivers / switching resistance
- **Abstract summary:** Despite the strategic importance of retaining customers, firms lack a clear framework for leveraging brand benefits to minimize actual switching behavior. This paper develops and tests a model examining how specific brand benefit categories activate customer resistance to switching. The findings offer actionable levers for managers seeking to fortify brand loyalty against competitive entry.
- **Methodology:** Experimental and/or survey-based (multi-study design implied by JAMS publication norms; specific methods not fully specified in available abstract)
- **Key findings:** The key benefits a brand provides to customers are central levers for reducing actual switching behavior and enhancing resistance to switch; different benefit types vary in their retention efficacy.
- **Research gap signaled:** Dynamic switching resistance over time; how digital touchpoints and AI-driven personalization alter the brand–switching calculus.
- **Data source used:** Primary survey / experimental data
- **Relevance:** ★★★★★

### The Effects of Dual-Oriented Branding Strategies on Brand Equity Through Innovation Investment
- **Authors:** [Authors not retrieved from available sources — see DOI for full attribution]
- **Journal:** Journal of Business Research | **Year:** 2025
- **Volume:** Vol. 190
- **DOI:** 10.1016/j.jbusres.2025.00058X  
  *(Verify exact DOI at: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S014829632500058X)*
- **Focus:** Branding strategy → firm innovation → brand equity chain
- **Abstract summary:** This study introduces a dual classification of branding strategy — organization-oriented and market-oriented — and investigates how each influences innovation investment and, in turn, brand equity. A mixed-methods design combining qualitative text analysis with quantitative survey data from publicly listed Chinese enterprises is used to test the mediation model. Executive mindset (long-term, innovation-oriented) is identified as a facilitating condition.
- **Methodology:** Mixed methods — qualitative text analysis of corporate materials + quantitative survey from firms listed on Shanghai and Shenzhen stock exchanges (archival + primary data)
- **Key findings:** Both organization-oriented and market-oriented branding strategies positively drive innovation investment, which in turn enhances brand equity; executive long-term orientation moderates this relationship.
- **Research gap signaled:** Generalizability beyond Chinese listed firms; effects in B2B contexts; role of brand strategy type in digital-native vs. traditional firms.
- **Data source used:** ⚑ Archival/secondary data (Shanghai/Shenzhen stock exchange disclosures) + primary survey — publicly available financial disclosures used
- **Relevance:** ★★★★

### Investigating the Transformative Power of Brand Love in Destination Crisis Recovery
- **Authors:** Zhang, Hui; Zhu, Hong (Sun Yat-sen University)
- **Journal:** Journal of Travel Research | **Year:** 2026
- **DOI:** 10.1177/00472875241296086
- **Focus:** Brand crisis recovery / brand love as recovery mechanism
- **Abstract summary:** This study examines how brand love functions as a transformative asset in helping destination brands recover from public health crises. Drawing on affective attachment theory, the paper traces how pre-crisis brand love moderates the severity of brand equity erosion and accelerates post-crisis tourist re-engagement. Implications are drawn for destination marketing organizations managing reputational recovery.
- **Methodology:** Quantitative survey design (specific modeling approach not fully confirmed; published in JTR suggesting empirical/structural model)
- **Key findings:** Brand love acts as a buffer during destination crises and a catalyst for recovery, significantly moderating the negative impact of public health crises on destination brand equity.
- **Research gap signaled:** Whether brand love effects generalize to product and service brands (beyond destination brands); long-term tracking of recovery trajectories.
- **Data source used:** Primary survey data
- **Relevance:** ★★★  
  *(Note: Journal of Travel Research is outside the primary target journal list but findings directly inform brand crisis/recovery research stream)*

### Keeping It in the Family: Measures and Drivers of Portfolio Brand Cohesion
- **Authors:** Ward, Ella; Beal, Virginia; Dawes, John; Trinh, Giang; Romaniuk, Jenni
- **Journal:** Journal of Brand Management | **Year:** 2025 (published online July 19, 2025)
- **DOI:** 10.1057/s41262-025-00410-2
- **Focus:** Brand portfolio management / brand architecture / visual cohesion measurement
- **Abstract summary:** Despite the strategic importance of brand visual coherence, empirical measurement of portfolio-level cohesion has been limited. This study develops a Portfolio Brand Cohesion (PBC) measure and applies it to 125 brands and 1,023 products across nine packaged-goods categories in the UK. The drivers and inhibitors of cohesion are identified through regression analysis.
- **Methodology:** Archival/secondary data analysis — observational coding of brand visual elements across 9 UK packaged-goods categories, 125 brands, 1,023 SKUs
- **Key findings:** Average brand cohesion is 65% across the sample; larger portfolios, presence of sub-brands, and private-label status each significantly reduce portfolio cohesion.
- **Research gap signaled:** Impact of reduced cohesion on brand equity and sales outcomes; longitudinal dynamics as portfolios expand; applicability to digital/DTC brand architectures.
- **Data source used:** ⚑ Publicly observable secondary data — UK packaged-goods market (retail shelf/packaging data); no Interbrand or financial data, but uses archival observational coding
- **Relevance:** ★★★★
