### Signaling Sequel Success: How Franchise Characteristics Shape the Influence of Brand Consistency on Movie Sequel Performance
- **Authors:** Brandon Zachary Holle, Ahmet Halil Kirca, Jamal Shamsie, Daniel Chaffin
- **Journal:** Journal of the Academy of Marketing Science (JAMS) | **Year:** 2026 | **Status:** online-first
- **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1007/s11747-026-01148-2
- **Published online:** April 15, 2026
- **Abstract summary:** Drawing on signaling theory, this study examines how brand consistency functions as a quality signal for movie sequels. It argues that the signal value of brand consistency is contingent on franchise characteristics — specifically franchise length, age, and origin — that shape consumers' signal interpretation. Using a sample of 466 movie sequels, the study empirically tests how these characteristics moderate the brand consistency–box office performance relationship.
- **Methodology:** Secondary data analysis (N = 466 movie sequels); moderated regression
- **Key findings:** Brand consistency positively predicts box office revenue, but its effect is moderated by franchise length, age, and origin (e.g., the signal is weaker for long-running or franchise-adapted properties). Longer franchises with established equity benefit less from pure consistency signaling.
- **Research gap signaled:** Future research should explore brand consistency signals in other entertainment categories (e.g., streaming, gaming) and cross-cultural franchise performance; brand extensions beyond entertainment media.
- **Relevance:** ★★★★☆ — Directly extends brand consistency and brand signaling theory in a high-stakes product category; strong empirical contribution to brand extension/franchise branding literature.

### AI vs. Human Models in Fashion Brand Advertising: A Schema Theoretical Perspective on Consumer Responses
- **Authors:** Sanghee Kim
- **Journal:** Journal of Retailing and Consumer Services (JRCS) | **Year:** 2026 | **Status:** online-first (Vol. 92, June 2026, in press)
- **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jretconser.2026.104820
- **URL:** https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0969698926001001
- **Published online:** est. late March – early April 2026 (2026 DOI confirmed)
- **Abstract summary:** Drawing on schema theory, this study examines how using AI-generated versus human models in fashion brand social media advertisements affects advertising skepticism, brand advocacy, and behavioral intentions. Brand type (luxury vs. non-luxury) is tested as a moderator. Data from 875 US respondents reveal that consumers hold systematically negative schemata toward AI models linked to job displacement concerns, ethical issues, and perceived devaluation of brand effort.
- **Methodology:** Online survey experiment (N = 875, US respondents); 2 (model type: AI vs. human) × 2 (brand type: luxury vs. non-luxury) between-subjects design
- **Key findings:** AI models significantly increase advertising skepticism, which in turn suppresses brand advocacy, social media WOM intention, and purchase intention. Luxury brands suffer a stronger negative effect. Human models elicit more favorable brand-related outcomes across the board.
- **Research gap signaled:** Future research should examine AI model use across cultures, product categories beyond fashion, and longitudinal normalization of AI model acceptance; also brand authenticity perceptions when AI content is disclosed vs. undisclosed.
- **Relevance:** ★★★★★ — Highly relevant to digital brand strategy and AI-driven branding; directly tests brand perception mechanisms in the context of generative AI advertising content.

### Do Second-Hand Luxury Retailers Affect Luxury Brand Image? Exploring the Mediation of Construal Level Theory Between Official and Unofficial Retailers
- **Authors:** Bai, Huifeng; Tassiello, Vito; Sun, Yan; Shi, Jin
- **Journal:** Journal of Retailing and Consumer Services (JRCS) | **Year:** 2026 | **Status:** published (Vol. 88, January 2026)
- **URL:** https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0969698925003121
- **Abstract summary:** This study investigates whether purchasing a second-hand luxury product via official or unofficial retailer channels affects perceptions of luxury brand image, and whether construal level mediates this effect. Two online experiments test how retailer type activates high vs. low psychological construal, which in turn shapes brand image perceptions.
- **Methodology:** Two online experiments; construal level theory framework; mediation analysis
- **Key findings:** Purchasing from official second-hand retailers activates high-level construal (focus on brand identity/values), preserving brand image. Unofficial retailers activate low-level construal (focus on tangible product attributes), degrading brand image. Brand managers should carefully control second-hand distribution channels.
- **Research gap signaled:** Future work should examine how brand communication strategies can re-frame construal for unofficial channel consumers; extend to non-luxury categories and cultural contexts (China, Japan).
- **Relevance:** ★★★★☆ — Directly addresses luxury brand image management in secondary markets; strong implications for digital brand economy and platform brand strategy.

### Fashion Retailing and Consumers in the Digital Age: E-Semiotics and the Co-Creation of Luxury Brand Narratives on Instagram
- **Authors:** Naeem, Muhammad
- **Journal:** Journal of Retailing and Consumer Services (JRCS) | **Year:** 2026 | **Status:** online-first (Vol. 90, March 2026, in progress)
- **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jretconser.2025.104695
- **URL:** https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0969698925004746
- **Abstract summary:** This mixed-methods study examines how consumers co-create luxury brand identity through e-semiotic symbolic interactions on Instagram, and how these interactions affect brand love. Three luxury brands (Stella McCartney, Alexander McQueen, Burberry) were analyzed through content analysis, in-depth interviews, and a quantitative survey, revealing the mechanisms by which digital semiotic practices build or erode brand meaning.
- **Methodology:** Mixed methods — 32 in-depth interviews, 24 focus group participants (6 groups), Instagram content analysis (3 luxury brands), quantitative survey (N = 342)
- **Key findings:** E-semiotic consumer interactions on Instagram meaningfully co-create luxury brand identity and significantly drive brand love. The brand retains narrative control only partially — consumer re-signification reshapes brand meaning in ways brands cannot fully manage.
- **Research gap signaled:** Future research should examine brand narrative co-creation across other platforms (TikTok, Pinterest), and the role of AI-generated content in diluting or amplifying brand semiosis; cross-cultural luxury brand communities.
- **Relevance:** ★★★★☆ — Directly relevant to digital branding, brand co-creation, and luxury brand community; strong link to digital brand equity and platform brand topics.

### Branded, Partnered, or Opting Out: A Comparison of Secondhand Business Models in Shaping Consumer Responses
- **Authors:** Shin, Daeun Chloe; Lee, Angie; Lee, Garim
- **Journal:** Journal of Retailing and Consumer Services (JRCS) | **Year:** 2026 | **Status:** online-first (2026 in-progress volume)
- **URL:** https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0969698925004205
- **Abstract summary:** As secondhand retail becomes integral to fashion's sustainability shift, this study compares three secondhand business model structures: (1) traditional (new products only), (2) dual-branded (brand sells new and secondhand), and (3) dual third-party (brand sells new; secondhand via a partner brand). Two experiments examine how model structure shapes perceived environmental commitment, brand attitudes, and downstream consumer behavior.
- **Methodology:** Two online experiments; between-subjects design comparing three secondhand business model conditions
- **Key findings:** Dual-branded models (brand controls both channels) generate the highest perceived environmental commitment and most favorable brand attitudes. Third-party secondhand partnerships yield intermediate effects. Perceived environmental commitment fully mediates the model structure–brand attitude relationship.
- **Research gap signaled:** Future research should explore co-branding dynamics in secondhand partnerships (e.g., brand-to-brand trust spillover), examine cross-category effects (electronics, furniture), and test interactions with brand heritage and authenticity perceptions.
- **Relevance:** ★★★☆☆ — Relevant to brand partnership/co-branding strategy and sustainable brand identity; applicable to digital platform brand management in circular economy contexts.
