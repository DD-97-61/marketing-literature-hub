### Brand equity and corporate social irresponsibility: evidence from an emerging market
- **Authors:** Hou, S., Guo, Z., Sun, X.
- **Journal:** Journal of Brand Management | **Year:** 2026
- **DOI:** 10.1057/s41262-026-00430-6
- **Focus:** brand equity / brand resilience / brand crisis
- **Abstract summary:** Using panel data from Chinese listed firms, this study examines how brand equity influences corporate social irresponsibility (CSI). Drawing on prospect theory, the authors investigate the brand equity–CSI relationship through risk- and pressure-based mechanisms, revealing conditions under which stronger brands may paradoxically be associated with more socially irresponsible behavior.
- **Methodology:** Panel data regression (archival/secondary data from Chinese listed firms); prospect theory framework; moderation analysis.
- **Key findings:** Brand equity exhibits a U-shaped relationship with CSI. Higher managerial ownership steepens this effect, while greater strategic deviance and stronger government intervention flatten it.
- **Research gap signaled:** Future research should explore cross-cultural/institutional variation in brand–CSI dynamics and investigate how brand equity interacts with other governance mechanisms to shape corporate ethical behavior.
- **Data source used:** Financial/archival data (Chinese listed firms panel dataset); brand equity proxies from secondary sources.
- **Relevance:** ★★★★☆

### Accessing the Untapped Brand Leverage Potential: A Strategic Framework from a Capital Market View
- **Authors:** Marc Fischer, Simone Wies
- **Journal:** Management Science | **Year:** 2025 | **Volume:** 71(3) | **Pages:** 2011–2034
- **DOI:** 10.1287/mnsc.2022.00670
- **Focus:** customer-based brand equity (CBBE) / financial brand equity / brand valuation
- **Abstract summary:** This paper investigates whether firms fully capitalize on the financial strength of their consumer-based brand equity (CBBE), and measures the gap to a benchmark where CBBE is financially fully leveraged. It proposes a "brand capabilities gap" (BCG) framework and identifies market strategy moderators that firms can steer to close the gap between actual and potential brand-driven firm value.
- **Methodology:** Capital market methodology; archival financial and brand equity data; strategic framework combining CBBE and firm value modeling.
- **Key findings:** Most firms leave substantial brand value on the table. Three market strategy moderators (identified in the framework) are actionable levers for closing the brand capabilities gap and unlocking latent CBBE value at the capital market level.
- **Research gap signaled:** Authors call for longitudinal studies tracking brand capabilities gap evolution, and for research extending the framework to service-dominant and platform-based brand contexts.
- **Data source used:** Capital market data (stock returns, firm financials); consumer-based brand equity data (secondary/syndicated).
- **Relevance:** ★★★★★ — *flags publicly available financial + CBBE data; high archival replication potential*

### Non-Fungible Tokens (NFTs) as digital brand extensions: Evidence on financial performance and parent-brand spillovers
- **Authors:** Leif Brandes, Katharina Dölp
- **Journal:** International Journal of Research in Marketing | **Year:** 2025 | **Volume:** 42(3) | **Pages:** 491–521
- **DOI:** 10.1016/j.ijresmar.2025.01.001 (ScienceDirect: S0167811625000023)
- **Focus:** digital brand assets / NFT brand / digital brand valuation
- **Abstract summary:** This study investigates how global brands use non-fungible tokens (NFTs) as digital brand extensions. Using a dataset of 450 NFT campaigns, the authors document whether traditional brand extension factors and NFT-specific value drivers predict financial revenues and whether such campaigns create spillovers for the parent brand.
- **Methodology:** Archival analysis of 450 NFT campaigns; regression modeling combining traditional brand extension variables (brand equity, extension fit) and NFT-specific factors (utility, Web3 partnerships, campaign size).
- **Key findings:** Both brand strength/image and NFT-specific factors (e.g., Web3 expert partnerships, added utility beyond the token) predict NFT campaign revenues. However, NFT launches can create negative spillovers for the parent brand's consumer attitudes, requiring careful brand extension management.
- **Research gap signaled:** Future work needed on long-term brand equity effects of NFT campaigns, and on NFT strategy in luxury vs. mass-market brand contexts.
- **Data source used:** Archival NFT campaign dataset (450 campaigns); secondary brand equity and financial data.
- **Relevance:** ★★★★★ — *directly addresses digital brand assets; uses archival/secondary data*

### Do non-fungible token (NFT) initiatives create market value for firms?

- **Journal:** Journal of Retailing | **Year:** 2025
- **DOI:** S0022435925001186
- **Focus:** digital brand assets / NFT brand / financial brand equity / digital brand valuation
- **Abstract summary:** This study uses the event study method to examine how firms' NFT announcements affect their stock returns, analyzing a sample of 135 NFT announcements by publicly traded U.S. companies. The paper contributes to the nascent literature on retailing and NFTs and reveals investors' differential perceptions of NFT types (e.g., NFT platforms vs. branded NFT collectibles).

- **Research gap signaled:** Future research should investigate long-term value creation from NFT portfolios, and should examine non-U.S. markets and smaller brand contexts.
- **Data source used:** Stock market data (CRSP or similar); NFT announcement event data (archival).
- **Relevance:** ★★★★★ — *uses publicly available financial data; event study replication-friendly*

### The effects of dual-oriented branding strategies on brand equity through innovation investment
- **Authors:** Lefa Teng, Chenxin Xie, Xue Huang, Jun Ma
- **Journal:** Journal of Business Research | **Year:** 2025 | **Volume:** 190
- **DOI:** S014829632500058X
- **Focus:** brand equity / brand competitiveness / competitive advantage branding / brand differentiation
- **Abstract summary:** Drawing on internal (organization-oriented) and external (market-oriented) branding strategy lenses, this paper examines how dual-oriented branding strategies influence brand equity through the mediating mechanism of innovation investment. The study applies mixed methods to operationalize branding strategy dimensions and test the proposed model in Chinese firms.
- **Methodology:** Mixed methods (qualitative + quantitative); survey data; mediation analysis (innovation investment as mediator); moderation by executives' long-term and innovation-oriented mindsets.
- **Key findings:** Both organization-oriented and market-oriented branding strategies positively drive innovation investment, which in turn enhances brand equity. Executive mindset (long-term, innovation-oriented) amplifies this relationship.
- **Research gap signaled:** Future research should examine the dual-strategy model in Western contexts, across industries with different innovation cycles, and with longitudinal designs to track equity accumulation.
- **Data source used:** Survey (primary data from Chinese firms); qualitative interviews.
- **Relevance:** ★★★★☆

### Keeping it in the family: measures and drivers of portfolio brand cohesion
- **Authors:** [Not confirmed — article at DOI 10.1057/s41262-025-00410-2]
- **Journal:** Journal of Brand Management | **Year:** 2025/2026
- **DOI:** 10.1057/s41262-025-00410-2
- **Focus:** brand portfolio / brand architecture / brand hierarchy
- **Abstract summary:** This paper introduces a Portfolio Brand Cohesion Metric (PBCM) and applies it to the UK packaged-goods sector, covering nine product categories, 125 brands, and 1,023 products. It investigates what brand architecture factors drive or erode consistency of branding elements across a product portfolio.
- **Methodology:** Archival/secondary data on UK packaged-goods sector; measurement of visual brand cohesion across product portfolios; regression analysis of cohesion drivers.
- **Key findings:** On average, brands achieve 65% cohesion across branding elements. Sub-brands, private-label status, larger visual element counts, and larger portfolio size all significantly reduce cohesion.
- **Research gap signaled:** Future research should explore PBCM in service and digital brand portfolios, and examine whether higher cohesion causally improves brand equity or sales performance.
- **Data source used:** Archival/secondary data (UK packaged-goods sector; brand visual audits).
- **Relevance:** ★★★★☆ — *flags archival portfolio data source*

### Business-to-Business (B2B) branding: What we know and where do we go
- **Authors:** Debashree Roy Bhattacharjee, Debasis Pradhan, Kunal Swani
- **Journal:** Journal of Business Research | **Year:** 2026
- **DOI:** S0148296326001712
- **Focus:** brand equity / competitive advantage branding / digital brand assets (as emerging sub-theme)
- **Abstract summary:** This comprehensive systematic literature review analyzes over 150 primary B2B branding articles published over the past 25 years. It maps 10 recurring and emerging research areas across three eras of B2B branding scholarship, and identifies overlooked research gaps that could catalyze a new phase of B2B branding research.
- **Methodology:** Systematic literature review; bibliometric and thematic analysis; 25-year coverage of B2B branding research.
- **Key findings:** Ten key research areas identified, including digital and social media branding, sustainability branding, employer branding, and financial brand equity. Three author-defined eras reveal how B2B branding has matured. Multiple underexplored gaps identified with research questions and managerial implications.
- **Research gap signaled:** Digital/AI-augmented B2B brand management, cross-cultural B2B brand equity measurement, and financial returns to B2B brand investment are signaled as high-priority gaps.
- **Data source used:** Literature (systematic review); secondary/archival journal articles.
- **Relevance:** ★★★★☆

### Brand strategy in the metaverse: Insights from companies venturing into virtual environments

- **Journal:** Journal of Innovation & Knowledge | **Year:** 2025
- **DOI:** S2444569X25000605
- **Focus:** digital brand assets / metaverse brand / virtual brand goods / digital economy brand
- **Abstract summary:** This qualitative study examines how 16 large international corporations across diverse industries integrate the metaverse into their branding strategies. The paper identifies four distinct approaches to metaverse brand integration, ranging from exploratory pilots to fully committed virtual brand ecosystems, and evaluates their alignment with strategic brand objectives.
- **Methodology:** Qualitative case study analysis of 16 multinational corporations; content analysis of metaverse brand initiatives.
