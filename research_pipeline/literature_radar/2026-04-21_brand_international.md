### Beyond Adaptation: Consumer-Driven Glocalization and the Negotiation of Luxury Brand Meaning in China

- **Authors:** Yu K., He Z.
- **Journal:** Marketing Intelligence & Planning | **Year:** 2025 (ahead-of-print; DOI: 10.1108/MIP-04-2025-0319)
- **Geographic focus:** China (Generation Z consumers in Chinese digital spaces)
- **Brand/industry:** Western luxury brands operating in China; fashion and lifestyle sectors
- **Abstract summary:** Addresses the enduring challenge luxury brands face in balancing global consistency with local adaptation in digitally connected markets, foregrounding consumer agency rather than brand-side strategy. Combines large-scale netnographic analysis (3,500+ consumer posts) with 16 in-depth interviews to examine how Chinese Gen Z consumers evaluate cultural authenticity, brand heritage, and digital engagement in localized luxury campaigns. Develops a four-dimension conceptual framework for consumer-driven luxury brand glocalization.
- **Methodology:** Qualitative mixed-method — netnography (social media data, 3,500+ posts) + 16 in-depth interviews + thematic analysis. **FLAG: uses secondary social media data.**
- **Key findings:** Glocalization success is not determined by brand strategy alone but is co-constructed through consumer interpretation and digital discourse; identifies four interrelated dimensions: (1) cultural authenticity, (2) balance of brand heritage and local adaptation, (3) consumer digital engagement, and (4) consumer-driven brand equity.
- **Research gap signaled:** Consumer digital activism as a force shaping brand desirability; role of "fluid consumers" who navigate global and local identities simultaneously; Gen Z as active cultural curators rather than passive audiences.
- **China relevance:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ — Directly investigates Western luxury brand glocalization as experienced by Chinese Gen Z consumers using real digital discourse data. Extends cultural branding, hybrid branding, and co-creation theory in a China context.
- **Relevance:** ★★★★★

### Research on The Internationalization Configuration Path of Chinese Brands: Considering Three Types of Globalizing Brands

- **Authors:** Junfeng Liao, Baojie Fu, Xinming He, Minru Yang
- **Journal:** SSRN Working Paper (SSRN Abstract ID: 5819532) | **Year:** November 28, 2025
- **Geographic focus:** Chinese brands expanding into overseas markets globally
- **Brand/industry:** Cross-industry; 172 Chinese brands across goods, services, and culture categories
- **Abstract summary:** Examines the internationalization paths of 172 Chinese brands operating overseas in the digital economy era, distinguishing among goods-type, services-type, and culture-type brands. Integrates natural language processing, fuzzy set qualitative comparative analysis (fsQCA), and multiple regression to reveal configurational pathways. Finds that cross-cultural adaptability of brand personality profiles significantly influences overseas market recognition efficiency.
- **Methodology:** Mixed-methods — NLP + fsQCA + multiple regression on a sample of 172 Chinese overseas brands. **FLAG: uses secondary/public data (brand database and NLP-extracted text data).**
- **Key findings:** Configurational effects of technological innovation capability, market expansion capability, and relational embeddedness jointly drive diversified internationalization pathways; no single dominant path — goods, service, and culture brands follow distinct configurations.
- **Research gap signaled:** Differentiated pathways for different brand type archetypes; role of brand anthropomorphism and brand empowerment in overseas contexts; cultural vs. functional branding strategies for Chinese firms.
- **China relevance:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ — Core 品牌出海 research. Directly maps the configuration logic behind Chinese brand internationalization across sectors, with actionable framework for practitioners.
- **Relevance:** ★★★★★
