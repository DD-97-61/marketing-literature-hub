### AI Storytelling for Global Consumers: Does It Improve Luxury Brand Experience and What Is the Role of Culture?
- **Authors:** Olga Nechaeva, Costanza Dasmi
- **Journal:** International Marketing Review | **Year:** 2026
- **Published online:** March 30, 2026
- **Geographic focus:** Cross-national (multi-country, comparative)
- **Brand/industry:** Luxury goods; AI-powered brand storytelling platforms
- **Abstract summary:** This study investigates how AI-based storytelling shapes the luxury brand experience for global consumers, using a luxury brand's own AI storytelling platform as stimulus material. The authors adopt an exploratory qualitative approach using 30 semi-structured interviews to unpack how consumers across cultures respond to AI-generated brand narratives. The paper introduces five distinct dimensions through which AI storytelling influences luxury brand experience—intellectual, affective, behavioral, sensory, and cultural—while foregrounding the moderating complexity of culture.
- **Methodology:** Qualitative — 30 semi-structured interviews; stimulus-based (AI storytelling platform); thematic analysis. No secondary/public data.
- **Key findings:** AI storytelling simultaneously enhances and creates friction within each of the five luxury brand experience dimensions; the cultural dimension emerges as both a mediator and moderator, with consumers' cultural backgrounds shaping receptivity to AI narrative authenticity.
- **Research gap signaled:** Future research should examine how culture-specific values (e.g., individualism vs. collectivism, power distance) interact with AI-generated brand stories; longitudinal work needed on how AI storytelling shifts brand equity over time across markets.
- **China relevance:** Moderately relevant — the cultural dimension finding speaks directly to how Chinese consumers (with Confucian/collectivist values and high digital AI adoption) may respond differently to luxury brand AI storytelling. Chinese luxury brand exporters (e.g., Shang Xia, NIO Life) could leverage or face challenges from this dynamic. **Flag for follow-up.**
- **Relevance:** ★★★★☆

### Exploring Purchase Intention in Cross-Border E-Commerce: A Relational View
- **Authors:** Ugné Wistedt, Aswo Safari
- **Journal:** International Marketing Review | **Year:** 2026
- **Published online:** April 2, 2026
- **Geographic focus:** Cross-border (consumer-side study; specific markets not reported in search results)
- **Brand/industry:** Retail; cross-border e-commerce; smaller "partial online internationalization" (POI) retailers
- **Abstract summary:** This paper adopts relational exchange theory and consumer attitude literature to examine purchase intention toward smaller country-specific retailers operating in cross-border e-commerce (CBEC). Using a survey of 364 respondents exposed to a real-life POI-retailer, the study maps the relational dynamics of consumer CBEC purchasing under uncertainty. The focus on POI-retailers (as opposed to large platform giants) fills a gap in e-commerce internationalization research.
- **Methodology:** Quantitative survey (n=364); stimulus-based experimental design with real POI-retailer exposure; structural equation modelling inferred. Uses primary survey data.
- **Key findings:** Uncertainty negatively affects consumer trust but not attitude; counterintuitively, purchase intention is primarily driven by consumer attitude and commitment—trust has an insignificant effect on both commitment and purchase intention toward unfamiliar POI-retailers.
- **Research gap signaled:** Future research should test this model with POI-retailers from different country-of-origin contexts; longitudinal and behavioral (transaction) data needed to validate self-reported purchase intentions.
- **China relevance:** **High relevance.** Chinese brands frequently enter global markets as exactly this type of POI-retailer (e.g., country-specific brand websites, DTC storefronts) before scaling to platforms. The finding that attitude (not trust) drives intent suggests Chinese brands may benefit from aggressive brand positioning and visual/social identity investment even before trust-building. Uses e-commerce data context — **flagged for secondary data note.**
- **Relevance:** ★★★★★

### The International Expansion of Digital Platforms: A Dynamic Setting for Challenging and Advancing Theories of the Multinational Enterprise
- **Authors:** Esteban García-Canal, Mauro F. Guillén
- **Journal:** Business Research Quarterly | **Year:** 2026 | **Vol.:** 29(1), pp. 62–77
- **Geographic focus:** Global; platform economy (no single geographic focus; multiple platform types discussed)
- **Brand/industry:** Digital platforms (broker vs. provider); technology sector; MNE theory
- **Abstract summary:** This conceptual article argues that the international expansion of digital platforms constitutes a fundamentally new setting for challenging and extending existing theories of the multinational enterprise. The authors distinguish between broker platforms (which scale globally with light infrastructure by leveraging network effects) and provider platforms (which require substantial local investment, regulatory compliance, and adaptation). The paper examines competitive strategies, governance mechanisms, and non-market strategies through this broker/provider lens.
- **Methodology:** Conceptual/theoretical review; no primary or secondary data collection. Framework development drawing on IB theory.
- **Key findings:** Platform internationalization mirrors born-global patterns but operates through novel mechanisms (network effects, data flywheel, ecosystem governance) distinct from traditional FDI logic; non-market strategies (lobbying, regulatory engagement) are disproportionately important for provider platforms expanding internationally.
- **Research gap signaled:** Empirical tests of the broker/provider distinction across firm performance; how platform home-country institutions shape cross-border governance choices; role of data localization laws in fragmenting platform internationalization.
- **China relevance:** **Very high relevance.** Chinese digital platforms (TikTok/ByteDance, Temu/PDD, Shein, Alibaba International, WeChat) are central cases of provider and broker platform internationalization. The regulatory-engagement finding maps directly onto ByteDance's non-market strategies in the US and EU. Excellent theoretical scaffolding for 品牌出海 in the digital platform context.
- **Relevance:** ★★★★★

### Brand Localization versus Globalization: A Comparative Analysis of Adaptation Strategies in Diverse Markets
- **Authors:** Not individually named in search results (multi-author; institutional affiliation from IntechOpen)
- **Journal:** IntechOpen (online-first chapter/edited volume) | **Year:** 2026 (online-first; exact date pending assignment to volume)
- **Geographic focus:** Six markets: United States, China, India, Germany, Brazil, Nigeria
- **Brand/industry:** Multinational firms across multiple industries (12 MNCs studied including FMCG, tech, apparel)
- **Abstract summary:** This comparative study examines how 12 multinational corporations navigate the standardization-adaptation tension across six culturally and economically diverse markets. Applying Ghemawat's CAGE framework (Cultural, Administrative, Geographic, Economic distances), the analysis identifies five primary brand dimensions subject to adaptation decisions—brand promise and core verbal architecture, visual architecture, product attributes, pricing, and promotional channels. The paper yields three clear adaptive strategies, with the most resilient brands using a "core standardization with peripheral adaptation" hybrid approach.
- **Methodology:** Qualitative comparative case study (12 MNCs); CAGE framework applied; no large-scale secondary dataset, but uses company documents and market analysis. **Uses secondary/public data (company reports, market data) — flagged.**
- **Key findings:** The most resilient global brands standardize central touchpoints (logo, brand promise, key messages) while selectively adapting peripheral features; the locus of adaptation hinges on offering type, market structural characteristics, and consumer psychographic profile.
- **Research gap signaled:** Longitudinal studies tracking how the balance between standardization and adaptation shifts over the brand lifecycle; quantitative validation of the three identified strategies across larger MNC samples.
- **China relevance:** China is one of the six focal markets, making this directly applicable to understanding how brands adapt (or should adapt) when entering or competing in China, and by analogy, how Chinese brands might calibrate standardization/adaptation when going global. **Flagged — China included as research context.**
- **Relevance:** ★★★★☆
