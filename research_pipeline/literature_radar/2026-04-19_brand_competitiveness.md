### Signaling Sequel Success: How Franchise Characteristics Shape the Influence of Brand Consistency on Movie Sequel Performance

- **Authors:** Brandon Zachary Holle, Ahmet Halil Kirca, Jamal Shamsie, Daniel Chaffin
- **Journal:** Journal of the Academy of Marketing Science (JAMS) | **Year:** 2026
- **Published online:** ~April 15, 2026 | **DOI:** 10.1007/s11747-026-01148-2
- **Focus:** Brand positioning / brand consistency / brand signaling / entertainment branding
- **Abstract summary:** The study applies signaling theory to explain how brand consistency functions as a quality signal for movie sequels, and how the strength of that signal is moderated by franchise characteristics. It empirically tests whether franchise length, franchise age, and franchise origin (Hollywood vs. non-Hollywood) amplify or attenuate the brand consistency–box office performance relationship. The paper contributes to both entertainment marketing and broader branding literatures.
- **Methodology:** Archival/secondary data. Sample of N=466 movie sequels. Regression-based moderation analysis. Theoretical grounding in signaling theory and brand equity theory.
- **Key findings:** Brand consistency is positively associated with opening weekend box office revenue. Longer franchises show a reinforced brand consistency effect due to cumulative brand-building across prior products. Franchise age and origin further moderate when brand consistency translates into commercial performance.
- **Research gap signaled:** When and under what conditions does brand consistency pay off? Future research could explore streaming/digital release contexts and cross-media franchise consistency.
- **Data source:** Archival/secondary data (movie box office records, franchise databases) — publicly available
- **Relevance:** ★★★★

### The Resilient Grass in the Storm: Brand Resilience in Uncertain Contexts

- **Authors:** Chunqing Li, Riyan Hao
- **Journal:** Journal of Brand Management | **Year:** 2026
- **Published online:** March 26, 2026 | **DOI:** 10.1057/s41262-026-00432-4
- **Focus:** Brand resilience / brand crisis / brand recovery / scale development
- **Abstract summary:** Despite brand resilience's managerial importance for navigating adverse environments, theoretical development and measurement tools remain underdeveloped. This paper addresses that gap through a mixed-methods design: qualitative phase identifies structural dimensions of brand resilience; quantitative phase develops and validates a 10-item measurement scale using EFA and CFA procedures. The paper offers both academic clarification of the construct and a practitioner-ready evaluation instrument.
- **Methodology:** Mixed methods. Phase 1: in-depth interviews + grounded theory (qualitative). Phase 2: survey-based scale development via exploratory factor analysis (EFA) and confirmatory factor analysis (CFA).
- **Key findings:** Brand resilience comprises three dimensions: (1) brand anticipation, (2) brand adaptability, and (3) brand transcendence. A 10-item validated scale was finalized.
- **Research gap signaled:** Antecedents and consequences of each resilience dimension; cross-industry and cross-cultural validation; longitudinal examination of resilience dynamics post-crisis.
- **Data source:** Interview data + survey data
- **Relevance:** ★★★★

### Brand Equity and Corporate Social Irresponsibility: Evidence from an Emerging Market

- **Authors:** Hou, S., Guo, Z., Sun, X.
- **Journal:** Journal of Brand Management | **Year:** 2026
- **Published online:** February 23, 2026 | **DOI:** 10.1057/s41262-026-00430-6
- **Focus:** Brand equity / financial brand equity / competitive advantage / corporate governance
- **Abstract summary:** The paper examines how brand equity influences corporate social irresponsibility (CSI) using a risk- and pressure-based lens grounded in prospect theory. Drawing on panel data from Chinese listed firms, the study reveals a non-linear (U-shaped) relationship: both very low and very high brand equity firms are more prone to CSI. The analysis also identifies managerial and institutional moderators that reshape this relationship.
- **Methodology:** Panel data analysis of Chinese listed firms. Non-linear regression (U-shaped relationship test). Prospect theory as conceptual foundation. Moderation by managerial ownership, strategic deviance, and government intervention.
- **Key findings:** U-shaped relationship between brand equity and CSI — firms at both extremes of brand equity engage in more socially irresponsible behavior. The effect is steeper when managerial ownership is high, flatter when strategic deviance is higher or government intervention is stronger.
- **Research gap signaled:** Dual (enabling/constraining) role of brand equity in social performance; institutional mechanisms to curb brand-linked irresponsibility in emerging markets; replication in Western/developed market contexts.
- **Data source:** Archival/financial data from Chinese listed firms (publicly available financial and CSR reporting data) — **FLAGGED: uses secondary financial data to measure brand equity**
- **Relevance:** ★★★★★

### How Can Businesses Enhance Customer Resistance to Brand Switching?

- **Authors:** Liu, Y., Duffek, B., Eisingerich, A.B.
- **Journal:** Journal of the Academy of Marketing Science (JAMS) | **Year:** vol. 54, pp. 207–227 (2026 print; published online 2025)
- **DOI:** 10.1007/s11747-025-01115-3
- **Focus:** Brand loyalty drivers / brand switching / brand defense / customer retention
- **Abstract summary:** This study investigates how businesses can reduce customer brand switching by leveraging specific benefit types that brands provide. The research delineates and tests two psychological mechanisms—self-esteem enhancement and self-betrayal—through which brands' "enriching benefits" lead to heightened customer resistance to switching. Findings span diverse field settings and respondent populations.
- **Methodology:** Multiple field experiments across diverse respondent samples. Mediation analysis (self-esteem, self-betrayal as parallel mediators). Varied brand and product category contexts.
- **Key findings:** Brands' enriching benefits (those that enhance customers' sense of self) significantly boost resistance to switching by simultaneously elevating customer self-esteem and creating anticipated feelings of self-betrayal upon switching. Both mechanisms independently contribute to switching resistance.
- **Research gap signaled:** Long-term resilience of switching resistance under competitive entry; role of digital/social identity in enriching benefits; cross-cultural variation in self-betrayal sensitivity.
- **Data source:** Experimental survey data
- **Relevance:** ★★★★
