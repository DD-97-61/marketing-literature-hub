### Color of Status: Color Saturation, Brand Heritage, and Perceived Status of Luxury Brands

- **Authors:** Xinyue Zhou, Chunqu Xiao, Sunyee Yoon, Hong Zhu
- **Journal:** Journal of Consumer Research (JCR) | **Year:** 2026 | **Status:** Published
- **Volume/Issue:** Vol. 52, Issue 6, pp. 1232–1252
- **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1093/jcr/ucaf029
- **Abstract summary:** This paper examines how product color saturation signals brand heritage and perceived status in luxury markets. Lower color saturation cues the passage of time, leading consumers to perceive the brand as having continuity heritage, which elevates status perceptions. Seven experimental and field studies confirm the mechanism and map its boundary conditions.
- **Methodology:** Seven experimental and field studies (lab experiments + field/real-product stimulus designs); within-subjects and between-subjects designs; willingness-to-pay and product choice as DVs.
- **Key findings:** Low (vs. high) color saturation increases perceived luxury brand status, mediated by perceived continuity heritage; the effect is attenuated when brands highlight recent founding years and reversed when brands position themselves as innovative.
- **Research gap signaled:** Future work could explore other perceptual cues (typography, texture) that signal temporal continuity, and how digital/screen rendering of color saturation may alter heritage perception in e-commerce contexts.
- **Relevance:** ★★★★★ — Directly advances the brand heritage and brand status literatures with a novel visual-cue mechanism; highly relevant to luxury brand strategy and visual brand identity research.

### Signaling Sequel Success: How Franchise Characteristics Shape the Influence of Brand Consistency on Movie Sequel Performance

- **Authors:** Brandon Zachary Holle, Ahmet Halil Kirca, Jamal Shamsie, Daniel Chaffin
- **Journal:** Journal of the Academy of Marketing Science (JAMS) | **Year:** 2026 | **Status:** Online-first (published ~April 15, 2026)
- **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1007/s11747-026-01148-2
- **Abstract summary:** Drawing on signaling theory, this study investigates how brand consistency influences box office performance for movie sequels and how this relationship is moderated by franchise characteristics. The authors argue that brand consistency functions as a quality signal whose potency depends on franchise length, age, and origin (original vs. adapted IP). Analyzing 466 movie sequels, they show that franchise context shapes whether consistency helps or hurts sequel performance.
- **Methodology:** Secondary data (archival); sample of N = 466 movie sequels; quantitative regression with moderation analysis; franchise-level panel structure.
- **Key findings:** Brand consistency positively predicts sequel box office performance; the effect is amplified for longer, older, and original (non-adapted) franchises—indicating that established brand signals carry greater credibility.
- **Research gap signaled:** Authors call for extension to other entertainment categories (video games, book series, streaming), international markets, and exploration of digital-era brand consistency signals (trailers, social media aesthetic coherence).
- **Relevance:** ★★★★☆ — Provides novel empirical evidence on co-branding dynamics within franchise structures; relevant to brand extension and brand fit literature, and extends signaling theory into the entertainment brand context.
