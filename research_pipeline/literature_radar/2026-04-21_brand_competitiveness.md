### 1. Non-Fungible Tokens (NFTs) as Digital Brand Extensions: Evidence on Financial Performance and Parent-Brand Spillovers

- **Authors:** Leif Brandes, Katharina Dölp
- **Journal:** International Journal of Research in Marketing | **Year:** 2025
- **Volume/Issue:** Vol. 42(3), pp. 491–521 | **Published:** January 17, 2025
- **Focus:** Digital assets / NFT brand / brand extension / digital brand valuation
- **Abstract summary:** Examines NFT campaigns as digital brand extensions, drawing on a novel archival dataset of 450 brand-initiated NFT campaigns. Tests whether established success drivers from physical brand extensions (brand equity, extension fit) and NFT-specific value drivers (utility, campaign size) predict revenues. Also provides experimental evidence on how NFT campaigns affect parent brand attitudes.
- **Methodology:** Archival analysis of 450 NFT campaigns (secondary financial and blockchain data) + experimental study
- **Key findings:** NFT-specific value drivers (added utility, NFT count) predict financial revenues; NFT campaigns can produce *negative* spillover effects on parent brand attitudes — a double-edged sword for brand managers. Brand–NFT fit and brand equity moderate outcomes.
- **Research gap signaled:** Longitudinal effects on parent brand equity; when digital brand extensions are net positive vs. net negative long-term; cultural boundary conditions
- **Data source used:** Archival — NFT campaign dataset (secondary), blockchain/sales data; experimental survey data
- **Relevance:** ★★★★★
- **FLAG:** Uses publicly available blockchain/campaign data to measure digital asset brand equity

### 2. The Effects of Dual-Oriented Branding Strategies on Brand Equity Through Innovation Investment

- **Authors:** Lefa Teng, Chenxin Xie, Xue Huang, Jun Ma
- **Journal:** Journal of Business Research | **Year:** 2025
- **Volume/Issue:** Vol. 190, Article 115250
- **Focus:** Brand competitiveness / brand equity / competitive advantage branding
- **Abstract summary:** Introduces a dual-oriented branding strategy framework that integrates organization-oriented branding (internal culture, identity) and market-oriented branding (external positioning, customer focus). Tests how this dual approach drives firm innovation investment and, in turn, enhances brand equity. Examines executive-level moderators.
- **Methodology:** Mixed-methods — qualitative text-mining of corporate reports + quantitative survey data from publicly listed firms on the Shanghai and Shenzhen stock exchanges
- **Key findings:** Both organization-oriented and market-oriented branding strategies independently and jointly increase innovation investment, which mediates the relationship with brand equity. Executive long-term and innovation orientations amplify these effects.
- **Research gap signaled:** Cross-cultural and cross-industry generalization; applicability to digital-native and SME contexts; dynamic capabilities lens
- **Data source used:** Financial/archival — Shanghai and Shenzhen Stock Exchange listed firm data; managerial survey
- **Relevance:** ★★★★☆
- **FLAG:** Uses archival financial + stock exchange data to link branding strategy to brand equity

### 3. Digital Brand Equity: The Concept, Antecedents, Measurement, and Future Development

- **Authors:** Stephen L. France, Nebojsa S. Davcik, Brett J. Kazandjian
- **Journal:** Journal of Business Research | **Year:** 2025
- **Volume/Issue:** Vol. 192
- **Focus:** Digital brand equity / brand valuation / digital assets / CBBE in digital era
- **Abstract summary:** Addresses the insufficiency of traditional brand equity metrics (Aaker, Keller CBBE) in the digital era. Develops a conceptual understanding of digital brand equity by synthesizing practitioner data, SEO/analytics literature, and scholarly brand equity research. Proposes a new set of digital-native metrics that capture brand equity in online environments.
- **Methodology:** Conceptual/review paper synthesizing practitioner views (digital analytics tools, SEO platforms) and scholarly literature
- **Key findings:** Proposes three novel digital brand equity constructs: (1) *share of search* (relative search interest as salience proxy), (2) *digital brand awareness* (cross-channel impression coverage), and (3) *digital brand sentiment* (social listening–derived valence). Current CBBE frameworks fail to capture digital-era brand dynamics.
- **Research gap signaled:** Empirical scale validation of digital BE constructs; cross-channel measurement; brand equity in platform/algorithmic environments; measurement across brand lifecycle stages
- **Data source used:** Conceptual — secondary/practitioner sources (Google Trends, social media metrics, SEO analytics)
- **Relevance:** ★★★★★
- **FLAG:** Explicitly proposes use of social media metrics, share-of-search, and digital analytics as publicly available brand equity data sources

### 4. Unlocking the Potential of NFTs in Branding: An Exploration of NFT-Based Brand Experience

- **Authors:** Wenjie Li, Graciela Corral de Zubielqui, Sally Rao Hill
- **Journal:** Journal of Retailing and Consumer Services | **Year:** 2025
- **Volume/Issue:** Vol. 85 | **Published:** May 3, 2025
- **Focus:** Digital brand assets / NFT brand / digital brand experience / Web3 brand
- **Abstract summary:** Explores the intersection of branding and NFTs through a typology-building approach. Identifies five distinct NFT functions that brands can deploy, and connects these to five types of brand experience design. Advances digital branding theory by theorizing how NFTs generate brand value beyond transactional utility.
- **Methodology:** Qualitative case-based typology development; conceptual framework
- **Key findings:** Five NFT functions: storytelling media, identity badges, product access passes, change medallions, and gamification elements. These map onto five brand experience designs: brand heritage, community building, product orientation, collaboration, and gamification. NFTs are powerful tools for community-driven brand equity.
- **Research gap signaled:** Empirical testing of the NFT brand experience typology; consumer-level measurement of NFT-driven brand equity; platform and token-type moderators
- **Data source used:** Qualitative — case evidence from real brand NFT deployments
- **Relevance:** ★★★★☆

### 5. How Can Businesses Enhance Customer Resistance to Brand Switching?

- **Authors:** Yeyi Liu, Barbara Duffek, Andreas B. Eisingerich
- **Journal:** Journal of the Academy of Marketing Science (JAMS) | **Year:** 2026
- **Volume/Issue:** Vol. 54(1), pp. 207–227
- **DOI:** 10.1007/s11747-025-01115-3
- **Focus:** Brand loyalty drivers / brand switching / brand defense / CBBE
- **Abstract summary:** Addresses the gap in understanding how brands mechanistically prevent customer switching. Articulates how enriching brand benefits (functional, social, hedonic, and self-expressive) reduce actual switching behavior. Proposes and tests psychological mechanisms—self-esteem maintenance and self-betrayal aversion—as the pathways through which brand benefits operate.
- **Methodology:** Multiple field studies and experiments across diverse consumer populations and industry contexts
- **Key findings:** Brands that offer "enriching benefits" reduce customer switching via two mechanisms: (1) customers feel that switching would undermine their self-esteem and (2) switching feels like betraying a meaningful relationship. Both mechanisms operate independently and interact. Effect sizes are substantial across service and product categories.
- **Research gap signaled:** Role of digital touchpoints in enriching benefit delivery; cross-cultural replication; how firm-level factors (brand investment, market share) moderate resistance; dynamic effects over customer lifetime
- **Data source used:** Primary — experimental and field study survey data
- **Relevance:** ★★★★★

### 7. The Influences of Branding on Personal Selling and Sales Management Strategies for the Digital Age

- **Authors:** Pantea Foroudi, Nektarios Tzempelikos, T.C. Melewar, Charles Dennis
- **Journal:** Journal of Business Research | **Year:** 2026
- **ScienceDirect:** https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0148296326002067
- **Special issue:** "Branding and Sales"
- **Focus:** Brand competitiveness / brand differentiation / brand positioning / digital brand
- **Abstract summary:** Develops a multilevel strategic branding framework linking organizational brand capabilities, salesperson-level brand identity, and customer-level brand outcomes in the digital era. Positions "strategic branding capabilities" as the central mechanism integrating internal brand culture with external sales effectiveness. Incorporates digital transformation readiness, sustainability orientation, and relational context as moderators.
- **Methodology:** Conceptual framework development; multilevel model
- **Key findings:** Strategic branding capabilities mediate the relationship between brand-driven sales strategies and sustainable performance outcomes. Key moderators include relational vs. transactional orientation, brand transparency, sustainability commitment, and market context (global vs. local). Digital transformation readiness amplifies brand-to-sales effectiveness.
- **Research gap signaled:** Empirical testing of multilevel brand-sales model; digital sales channel contexts; AI-assisted brand communication in sales; cross-cultural B2B applications
- **Data source used:** Conceptual — secondary literature review
- **Relevance:** ★★★☆☆

### 8. Keeping It in the Family: Measures and Drivers of Portfolio Brand Cohesion

- **Authors:** Ella Ward, Virginia Beal, John Dawes, Giang Trinh, Jenni Romaniuk
- **Journal:** Journal of Brand Management | **Year:** 2025
- **Pages:** 105–118
- **Focus:** Brand portfolio / brand architecture / brand hierarchy
- **Abstract summary:** Applies the Portfolio Branding Cohesion Metric (PBCM) to the UK packaged-goods sector to empirically examine how consistently brands deploy their visual identity elements across product portfolios. Investigates what structural brand and portfolio characteristics drive or undermine visual cohesion at scale.
- **Methodology:** Archival/observational — analysis of 125 brands across 9 categories and 1,023 products in UK packaged goods (secondary retail audit data)
- **Key findings:** On average, brands achieve 65% visual cohesion across their portfolio. Sub-brands, private labels, and a greater number of visual brand elements all significantly reduce portfolio cohesion. Large brand portfolios are hardest to keep visually unified.
- **Research gap signaled:** Optimal cohesion thresholds for brand equity outcomes; digital shelf and e-commerce cohesion measurement; international and service sector applications; consumer-level perception of cohesion vs. internal measurement
- **Data source used:** Archival — retail shelf audit data, brand visual element coding (publicly observable secondary data)
- **Relevance:** ★★★★☆
- **FLAG:** Uses publicly available brand and retail product data for archival brand portfolio analysis

### 9. Developing a Strategic Typology of Non-Fungible Tokens (NFTs)

- **Authors:** Achilleas Boukis, Mario Restuccia
- **Journal:** Journal of Marketing Management | **Year:** 2025
- **DOI:** 10.1080/0267257X.2025.2515075
- **Focus:** Digital assets / NFT brand / Web3 brand / tokenized brand / digital collectibles
- **Abstract summary:** Contributes to the nascent Web3 marketing stream by constructing a customer-centric strategic typology of NFTs as blockchain-enabled digital offerings. Grounds the framework in marketing strategy theory to guide brand managers in deploying NFTs strategically rather than opportunistically.
- **Methodology:** Conceptual typology development using a 2×2 matrix framework
- **Key findings:** NFTs vary along two strategic dimensions: *value on offer* (value-in-use vs. value-in-exchange) and *strategic focus* (transactional vs. relational). Four resulting NFT types: (1) Validation Certificates, (2) Digital Replicas, (3) Immersion Enablers, and (4) Digital Upgrades. Each type has distinct brand management implications, opportunities, and risks.
- **Research gap signaled:** Empirical consumer-level tests of each NFT type; brand-specific contingency factors; decentralized autonomous organization (DAO) branding; NFT lifecycle and secondary market effects on brand equity
- **Data source used:** Conceptual — secondary evidence from brand NFT case examples
- **Relevance:** ★★★★☆
