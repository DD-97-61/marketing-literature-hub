### Socially Fluent, Socially Awkward: Artificial Intelligence Relational Talk Backfires in Commercial Interactions
- **Authors:** Stephanie Kwari Dharmaputri, Anish Nagpal, Greg Nyilasy, Jing Lei (affiliations unconfirmed from search metadata; Nyilasy is at University of Melbourne)
- **Platform:** arXiv (cs.HC / marketing) | **Date posted:** April 14, 2026 ✅ (within last 7 days)
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.12206
- **Status:** Preprint
- **Research stream:** AI×brand
- **Abstract summary:** Examines how AI tools' growing social fluency (as deployed in Shopify, Klarna, Visa) affect consumer satisfaction when AI uses "relational talk" — informal, non-obligatory social communication embedded in transactional exchanges. Finds a negative main effect of AI relational talk on satisfaction, mediated by expectancy violation and perceived interaction awkwardness. Goal-relevant relational talk attenuates the negative effect.
- **Proposed methodology:** Controlled experiments; structural equation modeling (mediation); manipulation of AI relational talk modality.
- **Data source:** Experimental (lab/online participants); commercial AI platforms used as stimuli.
- **Key contribution claimed:** Challenges the widespread assumption that socially fluent AI improves customer satisfaction; identifies awkwardness as an emotional barrier to human-AI interaction in commerce.
- **Our assessment:** High relevance. Directly addresses a gap in AI×brand: most prior work assumes social fluency helps — this paper inverts that. Actionable for firms deploying AI chatbots. Methodologically clean experiments. Watch for journal submission track.
- **Relevance:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

### Efficiency of Proportional Mechanisms in Online Auto-Bidding Advertising
- **Authors:** Nguyen Kim Thang (affiliation unconfirmed)
- **Platform:** arXiv (cs.GT / econ) | **Date posted:** April 14, 2026 ✅ (within last 7 days)
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.12799
- **Status:** Preprint
- **Research stream:** Digital economy / methodology
- **Abstract summary:** Studies the efficiency of pure Nash equilibria under the liquid welfare objective for proportional mechanisms in auto-bidding advertising auctions. Establishes a tight Price of Anarchy (PoA) bound of 2 for the standard proportional mechanism and introduces a modified payment scheme achieving PoA of 1 + O(1)/(n−1).
- **Proposed methodology:** Game-theoretic analysis; equilibrium computation; Price of Anarchy bounds.
- **Data source:** Theoretical (no empirical data).
- **Key contribution claimed:** Tight characterization of efficiency loss in proportional auto-bidding mechanisms with a practically relevant modified scheme.
- **Our assessment:** Peripheral to brand strategy; highly relevant to digital advertising platform design and programmatic media buying. Useful background for teams working on ad-tech or brand spend optimization. Not a branding paper per se.

### ⚠️ Commercial Persuasion in AI-Mediated Conversations
- **Authors:** Francesco Salvi, Alejandro Cuevas, Manoel Horta Ribeiro (affiliations unconfirmed)
- **Platform:** arXiv (cs.SI / cs.CY) | **Date posted:** April 5, 2026 ⚠️ (13 days ago)
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.04263
- **Status:** Preprint
- **Research stream:** AI×brand / social media
- **Abstract summary:** Two preregistered experiments (N=2,012) comparing traditional search vs. LLM conversational agent for product selection from an eBook catalog. A fifth of products were randomly designated "sponsored." LLM-driven persuasion nearly triples sponsored product selection rate vs. traditional search (61.2% vs. 22.4%). Explicit "Sponsored" labels do not significantly reduce persuasion; concealing promotional intent makes influence nearly invisible (detection accuracy <10%).
- **Proposed methodology:** Preregistered between-subjects experiment; 5 frontier LLM models tested; detection accuracy and selection rate as outcomes.
- **Data source:** Experimental (online panel, N=2,012); public eBook catalog.
- **Key contribution claimed:** Empirical evidence that conversational AI can covertly redirect consumer choices at scale, and that existing transparency mechanisms (disclosure labels) are insufficient.
- **Our assessment:** Landmark paper for AI×brand and consumer protection. Findings are alarming for brand equity: sponsored placement becomes nearly undetectable. Critical read for brand strategy teams navigating AI-mediated commerce. Likely to attract significant press coverage and policy attention. High build-on potential.
- **Relevance:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐
