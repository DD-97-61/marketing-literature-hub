### How can businesses enhance customer resistance to brand switching?
- **Authors:** Liu, Yeyi; Duffek, Barbara; Eisingerich, Andreas B.
- **Journal:** Journal of the Academy of Marketing Science (JAMS) | **Year:** 2026 (Vol. 54, No. 1, pp. 207–227, February 2026)
- **DOI:** 10.1007/s11747-025-01115-3
- **Focus:** Brand loyalty / brand switching / brand defense
- **Abstract summary:** Addresses why and how brands' enriching benefits reduce actual customer switching behavior. The study theoretically delineates and empirically tests self-esteem and self-betrayal as the psychological mechanisms through which enriching brand benefits create resistance to switching across diverse field settings.
- **Methodology:** Multiple field studies combining field experiments and surveys; diverse respondent samples across industry contexts.
- **Key findings:** Enriching benefits offered by brands significantly reduce switching behavior; self-esteem and self-betrayal mediate this relationship, providing a novel psychological framework for brand defense strategy.
- **Research gap signaled:** Calls for further research on how different benefit types interact with switching contexts; also signals gaps in B2B settings and cross-cultural generalizability.
- **Data source:** Field experimental and survey data (primary consumer data)
- **Relevance:** ★★★★★
- **Note:** Published in JAMS — a top-tier target journal. Directly addresses brand loyalty/defense; strong relevance for competitive brand strategy research.

### The resilient grass in the storm: brand resilience in uncertain contexts
- **Authors:** Li, C.; Hao, R.
- **Journal:** Journal of Brand Management | **Year:** 2026
- **DOI:** 10.1057/s41262-026-00432-4
- **Focus:** Brand resilience / brand crisis / brand recovery
- **Abstract summary:** Addresses the theoretical and measurement gap in brand resilience research. Using mixed methods (qualitative grounded-theory interviews + quantitative scale validation), the study identifies conceptual dimensions of brand resilience and develops a validated 10-item measurement scale.
- **Methodology:** Mixed methods — qualitative phase (in-depth interviews + grounded theory) to identify dimensions; quantitative phase (exploratory and confirmatory factor analysis) to construct and validate the scale.
- **Key findings:** Brand resilience comprises three dimensions — brand anticipation, brand adaptability, and brand transcendence. The validated 10-item scale provides a measurement foundation for subsequent empirical work.
- **Research gap signaled:** Future research should examine antecedents and outcomes of brand resilience; cross-industry and longitudinal studies needed; potential links to financial brand value.
- **Data source:** Interview data (qualitative) + survey/scale validation data (quantitative)
- **Relevance:** ★★★★
- **Note:** Conceptual ambiguity and lack of measurement tools for brand resilience identified as critical barriers — this paper directly bridges that gap.

### Digital brand equity: The concept, antecedents, measurement, and future development
- **Authors:** France, Stephen L.; Davcik, Nebojsa S.; Kazandjian, Brett J.
- **Journal:** Journal of Business Research | **Year:** 2025 (Vol. 192)
- **DOI/PII:** S0148296325000967
- **Focus:** Digital brand equity / digital brand assets / digital economy brand
- **Abstract summary:** Develops a conceptual understanding of brand equity in digital environments, integrating practitioner and scholarly perspectives. Argues that digital brand equity cannot be adequately measured using only social media metrics and proposes three key digital constructs that capture human-brand-technology nuances.
- **Methodology:** Conceptual framework development + practitioner-focused analysis (secondary data: share of search, digital brand awareness, digital brand sentiment).
- **Key findings:** Digital brand equity requires three distinct constructs — share of search, digital brand awareness, and digital brand sentiment — and must account for human factors alongside technology-consumer dynamics. Current purely social-media-based metrics are insufficient.
- **Research gap signaled:** Lacks empirical validation at scale; calls for longitudinal tracking of digital brand equity metrics; future work needed on B2B digital brand equity and platform-specific measurement.
- **Data source:** Secondary/archival data — digital metrics (share of search, social media data, digital brand sentiment)
- **Relevance:** ★★★★★
- **Flag:** Uses publicly available digital data (share of search, social media metrics) to measure brand equity — directly relevant to archival/secondary data methodology.

### Customer-based brand competitiveness (CBBC) scale: advancing the relative perspective
- **Authors:** Wangmo, Gaki; Piehler, Rico; Baumann, Chris
- **Journal:** Journal of Product & Brand Management | **Year:** 2025 (Vol. 34, No. 4, pp. 450–)
- **DOI:** 10.1108/jpbm-04-2024-5076
- **Focus:** Brand competitiveness / competitive advantage branding / customer-based brand equity
- **Abstract summary:** Introduces customer-based brand competitiveness (CBBC) as a customer-perspective construct and develops a validated scale through a three-stage process combining qualitative consumer studies, expert interviews, and three quantitative studies spanning 5 industries and 1,157 consumers (2,078 brand evaluations).
- **Methodology:** Multi-stage scale development — Stage 1: literature review + qualitative consumer study (n=20); Stage 2: expert interviews (n=21) + survey; Stage 3: three quantitative consumer studies (n=1,157) across five industries; structural equation modelling for validation.
- **Key findings:** A parsimonious 7-item CBBC scale outperforms the existing brand competitiveness scale in explaining customer-based brand equity and purchase intentions. Advances the relative perspective of brand competitiveness over absolute brand strength metrics.
- **Research gap signaled:** Longitudinal and cross-national validation needed; future work on CBBC drivers (marketing spend, R&D, innovation); application to digital and platform brands.
- **Data source:** Consumer survey data (primary) across 5 industries
- **Relevance:** ★★★★★

### The effects of dual-oriented branding strategies on brand equity through innovation investment
- **Authors:** Teng, Lefa; Xie, Chenxin; Huang, Xue; Ma, Jun
- **Journal:** Journal of Business Research | **Year:** 2025 (Vol. 190, March 2025)
- **DOI:** 10.1016/j.jbusres.2025.115235
- **Focus:** Brand equity / competitive strategy / brand differentiation
- **Abstract summary:** Examines how dual-oriented branding strategies (organization-oriented + market-oriented) jointly influence firm innovation investment, which in turn enhances brand equity. Executives' long-term and innovation-oriented mindsets amplify the strategic-innovation investment relationship.
- **Methodology:** Quantitative study using archival/secondary firm-level data; moderated mediation analysis; tests executive mindset as boundary condition.
- **Key findings:** Both organization-oriented and market-oriented branding strategies positively enhance innovation investment; innovation investment mediates their impact on brand equity; executive mindsets (long-term, innovation-oriented) amplify the branding-innovation link.
- **Research gap signaled:** Future research on how dual-oriented strategies perform in SME vs. large firm contexts; digital-native firms; cross-country comparative analysis on executive mindsets.
- **Data source:** Archival/secondary data (firm-level financial and strategic data)
- **Relevance:** ★★★★
- **Flag:** Uses archival/secondary data to trace branding strategy → innovation → brand equity chain.

### Revolutionizing consumption: Unveiling the Allure of NFTs and digital twins for sustainable luxury fashion
- **Authors:** Massi, Marta; Vocino, Andrea; Piancatelli, Chiara; Cillo, Paola; Pellicelli, Anna Claudia
- **Journal:** Journal of Business Research | **Year:** 2026 (Vol. 202)
- **PII:** S0148296325005806
- **Focus:** NFT brand / digital brand assets / digital collectibles / blockchain brand / luxury brand competitiveness
- **Abstract summary:** Draws on dematerialization theory, institutional legitimacy theory, and the sufficiency model to examine how NFTs and digital twins influence consumer perceptions of sustainability, brand legitimacy, and purchase likelihood for luxury fashion. Uses a qualitative preliminary study followed by three controlled experiments.
- **Methodology:** Mixed methods — preliminary qualitative study + three experiments testing product type (non-NFT vs. NFT vs. digital twin) effects on purchase likelihood, sustainability perceptions, and brand legitimacy; mediation/moderation analysis.
- **Key findings:** Digital twin products (combining physical product + NFT) yield the highest purchase likelihood. The positive effect of NFTs is amplified when perceived product sustainability is high. Brand legitimacy serves as a key mediator.
- **Research gap signaled:** Need for longitudinal research on NFT brand equity accumulation; secondary market dynamics for branded NFTs; blockchain data-driven measurement of brand value in Web3; consumer segments for digital luxury adoption.
- **Data source:** Experimental (consumer data); qualitative interviews
- **Relevance:** ★★★★
- **Flag:** Directly addresses digital brand assets in the NFT/tokenization space — bridges luxury brand management with Web3.

### Digital marketing and brand competitiveness: The mediating role of marketing capability and operational capability
- **Authors:** Jia, Xingping
- **Journal:** Journal of Retailing and Consumer Services | **Year:** 2025 (Vol. 87)
- **DOI/PII:** S0969698925002085
- **Focus:** Digital economy brand / brand competitiveness / competitive advantage branding
- **Abstract summary:** Investigates how digital marketing influences brand competitiveness through marketing capability and operational capability as mediators, using panel data from Chinese listed companies (2012–2022). Tests boundary conditions related to ownership type and strategic emphasis.
- **Methodology:** Quantitative archival study — panel data from Chinese A-share listed companies (2012–2022); mediation analysis; ownership type and strategic emphasis as moderators.
- **Key findings:** Digital marketing exhibits a U-shaped association with marketing capability while positively affecting operational capability directly. Both capabilities significantly enhance brand competitiveness. Effects vary meaningfully by firm ownership type (SOE vs. private) and strategic emphasis.
- **Research gap signaled:** Cross-country replication needed; digital marketing–brand competitiveness link in service industries and SMEs; time-lagged effects on brand equity (CBBE vs. financial).
- **Data source:** Archival/secondary financial data — Chinese listed companies (publicly available, 2012–2022)
- **Relevance:** ★★★★
- **Flag:** Uses publicly available financial data (Chinese A-share listed firms) — relevant to archival methodology for brand competitiveness research.
