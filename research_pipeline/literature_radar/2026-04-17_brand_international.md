### Consumers' brand attitudes in a deglobalized world: external threats boost attitudes toward local brands through emotional bonding

- **Authors:** Hu Y., Xin Y.
- **Journal:** International Marketing Review | **Year:** 2026
- **Volume/Issue:** Vol. 43, No. 2, pp. 419–[x] | DOI: 10.1108/IMR-12-2024-0542
- **Geographic focus:** China (primary empirical context — trade-conflict era consumer behavior)
- **Brand/industry:** Domestic vs. foreign brands, cross-category; social media corpus of threatened brands
- **Abstract summary:** Examines how external threats triggered by deglobalization and trade conflicts reshape domestic consumers' brand attitudes. Uses qualitative analysis of social media data alongside experimental studies to show that external threats can activate emotional bonding as a psychological mechanism. When a brand faces trade-related threats (e.g., US–China tech restrictions), Chinese consumers rally around it—raising brand attitudes—unless the brand simultaneously signals strong innovation, which paradoxically dampens the rally effect.
- **Methodology:** Mixed — qualitative analysis of large-scale social media data (secondary/public) + multiple preregistered experiments. **Flags secondary data use.**
- **Key findings:** External threats significantly elevate domestic consumers' brand attitudes via emotional bonding; brand innovation information moderates this effect negatively (reducing the "rally" boost).
- **Research gap signaled:** Calls for more work on specific industry sectors, cross-national replication, and long-term longitudinal effects of deglobalization on brand loyalty.
- **China relevance:** ⭐ Directly studies Chinese consumer responses to brands under geopolitical/trade pressure (Huawei, etc.); highly actionable for Chinese brands navigating Western restrictions.
- **Relevance:** ★★★★★

### Navigating culture mixing: a framework for global brand legitimacy in an era of deglobalization

- **Authors:** Huang Q., Shi Z., Deng C.
- **Journal:** International Marketing Review | **Year:** 2025/2026 (ahead-of-print)
- **DOI:** 10.1108/IMR-09-2024-0355
- **Geographic focus:** Global/cross-national; conceptual framework applicable to brand entry in culturally distant markets
- **Brand/industry:** Global brands (cross-category) engaging in culture mixing (e.g., incorporating host-country cultural elements)
- **Abstract summary:** Proposes a dual-lens conceptual framework integrating the legitimation process perspective with the polyculturalist paradigm to help global brands navigate culture mixing under deglobalization. Identifies four adaptive culture-mixing strategies — material blending, heritage fusion, assimilation fusion, and value blending — each aligned with distinct phases of the legitimation process. The paper is a direct theoretical response to heightened cultural nationalism and "reverse globalization" tensions.
- **Methodology:** Conceptual/framework development (no primary data). Secondary literature synthesis.
- **Key findings:** Global brand legitimacy in a deglobalized world requires deliberate, stage-appropriate culture-mixing strategies; "assimilation fusion" (fully embedding host-culture symbolism) is most effective for advanced legitimation stages.
- **Research gap signaled:** Calls for empirical validation, particularly in emerging markets (China, India, Southeast Asia) and across luxury vs. mass-market brand contexts.
- **China relevance:** Framework is directly applicable to Chinese brands expanding into culturally distant markets (Europe, Middle East) and to foreign brands in China using 国潮 (guochao) elements. High relevance for 品牌出海 strategy.
- **Relevance:** ★★★★★

### The evolution of international marketing management: AI applications and emerging markets driving international selling in the digital era

- **Authors:** Le H.T.P.M., Nguyen P.V., Chebbi H., Dinh D.M.
- **Journal:** International Marketing Review | **Year:** 2025 (ahead-of-print)
- **DOI:** 10.1108/IMR-04-2025-0176
- **Geographic focus:** Multi-country; strong emerging-market thread (China, Southeast Asia, Africa)
- **Brand/industry:** Cross-sector; focus on international selling, platform-based retail, omnichannel
- **Abstract summary:** Systematic review of International Marketing and Management (IMM) literature published 1983–March 2025 (using bibliometric and thematic analysis). Identifies four dominant research clusters: (1) international management strategies, (2) market entry strategies (including Chinese market and cross-border M&A), (3) digital marketing strategies, and (4) internationalization and global expansion. The paper then maps five emerging AI application themes and proposes a forward-looking research agenda.
- **Methodology:** Systematic literature review (SLR) + bibliometric analysis. **Uses secondary/public data** (journal articles as data corpus).
- **Key findings:** AI is becoming a structuring force in international marketing research, particularly in customer engagement, brand co-creation, and market entry decision-making; emerging markets are increasingly the locus of theoretical innovation rather than just empirical testing sites.
- **Research gap signaled:** Calls for primary studies on AI-driven brand personalization across cultural contexts; sparse coverage of African markets.
- **China relevance:** Chinese market context explicitly identified as a key sub-theme; platforms like Alibaba/JD.com feature as exemplars of digital internationalization. Directly relevant to 品牌出海 digital platforms.
- **Relevance:** ★★★★

### The International Expansion of Digital Platforms: A Dynamic Setting for Challenging and Advancing Theories of the Multinational Enterprise

- **Authors:** García-Canal E., Guillén M.F.
- **Journal:** BRQ Business Research Quarterly | **Year:** 2026
- **DOI:** 10.1177/23409444251382952
- **Geographic focus:** Global; cross-regional comparison of platform expansion patterns
- **Brand/industry:** Digital platforms (e-commerce, ride-hailing, social media, fintech); includes TikTok/ByteDance, Alibaba, Amazon as implicit reference cases
- **Abstract summary:** Argues that digital platforms follow internationalization trajectories that fundamentally differ from traditional multinational enterprises (MNEs) — leveraging network effects, data assets, and ecosystem lock-in rather than tangible asset ownership. Draws a strategic distinction between "broker" platforms (connecting buyers and sellers) and "provider" platforms (actively managing logistics and content). Highlights regulatory adaptation and decentralized governance as critical theoretical gaps.
- **Methodology:** Conceptual/theoretical. Literature review and case illustration.
- **Key findings:** Broker vs. provider platform distinction drives divergent global expansion patterns; rapid scaling to achieve critical mass precedes (rather than follows) local legitimation — inverting classic Uppsala model predictions.
- **Research gap signaled:** Regulation-platform co-evolution across jurisdictions; governance of decentralized (Web3-enabled) platforms in international markets.
- **China relevance:** Highly relevant — TikTok/ByteDance, Shein, Temu, Pinduoduo all exemplify the "provider platform" internationalization model described. Useful theoretical lens for analyzing 品牌出海 via digital platform channels.
- **Relevance:** ★★★★

### Consumer attitudes toward brand supportive communication during geopolitical crises

- **Authors:** Kononov N., Linzen I.
- **Journal:** Journal of Business Research | **Year:** January 2026
- **Volume:** 203, Article 115798 | DOI: pii/S0148296325006216
- **Geographic focus:** Israel (field study context); generalizable cross-national experimental design
- **Brand/industry:** Global vs. local brands across consumer categories; 154 real brands in field study
- **Abstract summary:** Investigates how local consumers respond to solidarity/supportive messages from brands during active geopolitical crises. Across four preregistered experiments and a large-scale field study, finds that even minimal brand supportive communication ("solidarity messages") significantly improves consumer attitudes, especially toward global brands. Global brand messages are perceived as higher-risk, which paradoxically amplifies positive consumer evaluation when brands choose to speak.
- **Methodology:** Four preregistered experiments + field study (154 brands). Causal identification through experimental design.
- **Key findings:** Global brands benefit disproportionately from supportive crisis communication relative to local brands; perceived risk-taking mediates the effect; effect is robust across brand categories.
- **Research gap signaled:** Replication in non-conflict geopolitical contexts (trade wars, sanctions); long-term brand equity effects of crisis communication.
- **China relevance:** Provides actionable insight for Chinese brands in geopolitically sensitive markets (Europe, US): proactive brand communication during trade disputes may boost rather than harm consumer attitudes. Relevant to Huawei/DJI/BYD communication strategies.
- **Relevance:** ★★★

### The role of global marketing strategies on the international performance of SMEs: current state and future research agenda

- **Authors:** Mmadubuko M., Sousa C., Sadraei R., Kaufmann H.R.
- **Journal:** International Marketing Review | **Year:** 2025 (ahead-of-print)
- **DOI:** 10.1108/IMR-04-2025-0173
- **Geographic focus:** Multi-country; SME internationalization globally
- **Brand/industry:** Small and medium enterprises across manufacturing and service sectors
- **Abstract summary:** Systematic literature review of 42 high-impact articles on SME global marketing strategies. Synthesizes four dominant strategic orientations: (1) digital marketing and social media, (2) market niche exploration, (3) strategic adaptation and diversification, and (4) international networking. Identifies mediating/moderating variables (innovation capability, market knowledge, relational capital, institutional context) and proposes a structured future research agenda.
- **Methodology:** Systematic literature review (SLR) with thematic analysis. **Uses secondary/public data** (journal articles).
- **Key findings:** Digital marketing and networking strategies are the most consistently performance-enhancing for SME internationalization; market knowledge and institutional distance are critical boundary conditions.
- **Research gap signaled:** Lack of longitudinal studies; under-representation of African and Middle Eastern SMEs; need for studies on platform-mediated brand building by SMEs.
- **China relevance:** Many Chinese cross-border e-commerce players are SMEs; findings on digital marketing and niche strategies are directly applicable to early-stage 品牌出海 firms (e.g., Anker-style brands).
- **Relevance:** ★★★

### Culturally mixed co-branding product framing in China: the role of cultural sensitivity, product quality, and purchase probability

- **Authors:** [Authors per Palgrave Communications publication]
- **Journal:** Humanities and Social Sciences Communications (Palgrave/Nature) | **Year:** 2024
- **DOI:** 10.1057/s41599-024-02954-1
- **Geographic focus:** China
- **Brand/industry:** Consumer products; foreign global brands (FB) × Chinese patriotic brands (PB) co-branding
- **Abstract summary:** Studies two culturally mixed co-branded product (CMCP) framing strategies — "Foreign Brand × Patriotic Brand" (FB×PB) and "Patriotic Brand × Foreign Brand" (PB×FB) — under rising Chinese patriotism and guochao trends. Finds that which brand leads the co-branding frame determines whether cultural sensitivity or product quality dominates consumer evaluation. Rising patriotism makes local-first framing (PB×FB) more effective for cultural resonance, while foreign-first framing (FB×PB) is better for signaling premium quality.
- **Methodology:** Experimental study with Chinese consumers. Survey-based causal design.
- **Key findings:** PB×FB framing elevates cultural sensitivity perceptions; FB×PB framing elevates product quality perceptions; brand and product fit remain critical moderators for both frames.
- **Research gap signaled:** Cross-national replication; longitudinal tracking of guochao sentiment effects; digital (livestreaming) context for co-branded products.
- **China relevance:** ⭐ Directly studies Chinese brand co-branding dynamics under guochao. Extremely high relevance for any foreign brand entering China or any Chinese brand using joint-brand strategies for international markets.
- **Relevance:** ★★★★

### Strategic localization practices and glocalization dynamics: insights from the fashion industry

- **Authors:** [Fashion industry executive interview study, per Nature article]
- **Journal:** Humanities and Social Sciences Communications (Nature) | **Year:** August 2025
- **URL:** https://www.nature.com/articles/s41599-025-05542-z
- **Geographic focus:** Global fashion markets; European brand expansion to Asia and Americas
- **Brand/industry:** Fashion and apparel; focus on digital e-commerce and social media localization
- **Abstract summary:** Explores how fashion brands navigate the dual imperatives of global brand consistency and local cultural adaptation across digital channels. Based on 17 semi-structured interviews with fashion industry executives, the study identifies specific localization practices on e-commerce platforms and social media. Finds that cultural localization is a strategic necessity — not merely an operational adjustment — for fashion brand equity in foreign markets.
- **Methodology:** Qualitative — 17 semi-structured interviews with fashion industry executives. Thematic analysis.
- **Key findings:** Glocalization in fashion requires coordinated adaptation of language, visual identity, and influencer partnerships while preserving core brand DNA; digital touchpoints (Instagram, WeChat, TikTok) are the primary battleground for glocalization.
- **Research gap signaled:** Quantitative validation of identified glocalization practices; luxury vs. fast-fashion differences; emerging market–specific studies.
- **China relevance:** WeChat and TikTok/Douyin named as key localization channels; findings on digital glocalization directly applicable to fashion brands' 品牌出海 and to foreign brands entering China.
- **Relevance:** ★★★

2. **Culture mixing frameworks** — "Navigating culture mixing" (Huang et al.) provides a rare comprehensive conceptual model for how global brands legitimize themselves through local cultural elements — directly applicable to guochao-era China strategy.
3. **Digital platform internationalization** — García-Canal & Guillén (2026) offer a theoretical update to MNE theory that fits the TikTok/Shein generation of Chinese platform-enabled global brands perfectly.
4. **Chinese consumer psychology under external threats** — Hu & Xin's IMR paper is this cycle's standout for 品牌出海 relevance: it quantifies how trade-war narratives create emotional bonding between Chinese consumers and embattled domestic brands.
